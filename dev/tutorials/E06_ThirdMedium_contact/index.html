Tutorial 06: Third-medium contact

Source · Notebook · nbviewer

A slender column, clamped at both ends, is compressed by a rigid piston inside a box. It buckles, bends sideways until it touches the walls of the box, and keeps folding against them. The contact is not detected by any search algorithm: the empty space around the column is meshed and filled with a third medium, a very soft hyperelastic material that transmits almost no force while it is open and stiffens strongly when it is squeezed to zero thickness. This is the piston benchmark of Faltus, Amato and Horák (2026, Sec. 3.3), a hard test for the nonlinear solver because of the buckling and the snaps.

What you will learn

  • meshes with several subdomains (Triangulation(model, tags=...)) and a different material in each;
  • the third-medium contact method: ARAP2D + LogBarrier and the skeleton regularisation of the gradient jump (SkeletonTriangulation, jump, mean);
  • a robust Newton for problems that lose stability: the injectivity-preserving line search InjectivityLS with Armijo on the energy (energy=, injectivity=), and the hybrid Newton (HybridTangent, HessianRegularization sharing a TangentSwitch);
  • increments that grow when Newton converges easily (LoadStepping(grow=...));
  • reaction forces computed from the residual, and a force–displacement history written to a CSV file.

Measured on a Mac M5 Max (Julia 1.13.0, 4 threads, 4 BLAS threads; HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8; compiled backend), whole run including compilation: FAST 51 s (mesh Piston_1.msh, 1 340 free dofs, 15 increments, 22 s of them in solve!, mostly compilation); full 107 s (mesh Piston_0.5.msh, 5 494 free dofs, 400 increments, 900 Newton iterations, 78 s in solve!: 0.2 s per increment: a third in Newton, the rest in the Dirichlet projection, the updates of the compiled forms and the post-processing). One assembly of the tangent on the full mesh: 5.8 ms compiled, 31 ms with Gridap's lazy evaluation.

Problem statement

The box is $(0.1, 0.9)\times(0.1, 1.9)$ (plane strain, dimensionless lengths). The column is the strip $0.45 \le x \le 0.55$ of thickness $d = 0.1$ and length $L = 1.8$; the two regions on its sides are the third medium. The bottom of the column is clamped. The top of the box is the piston: it moves down by $u_y = \Lambda\,\bar u$, with the top of the column clamped to it and the third medium free to slide along it. The third medium slides on the bottom and on the side walls of the box.

Find $\boldsymbol u$ such that, for all $\boldsymbol v$,

\[\int_{\Omega_s} \partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi_s : \nabla_0 \boldsymbol v \,\mathrm dA + \int_{\Omega_{tm}} \partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi_{tm} : \nabla_0 \boldsymbol v \,\mathrm dA + \int_{\Gamma_{tm}} \frac{\sigma\beta}{h_F} [\![\nabla_0 \boldsymbol u]\!] : [\![\nabla_0 \boldsymbol v]\!] \,\mathrm ds = 0 ,\]

which is the stationarity condition of the total energy

\[\Pi(\boldsymbol u) = \int_{\Omega_s} \Psi_s \,\mathrm dA + \int_{\Omega_{tm}} \Psi_{tm} \,\mathrm dA + \frac12 \int_{\Gamma_{tm}} \frac{\sigma\beta}{h_F} [\![\nabla_0 \boldsymbol u]\!] : [\![\nabla_0 \boldsymbol u]\!] \,\mathrm ds .\]

Solid. A compressible Mooney–Rivlin material written for plane strain ($\mu_1 = \mu_2 = \mu$):

\[\Psi_s = \frac{\mu_1}{2}\left(\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F + 1 - 3\right) + \frac{\mu_2}{2}\left(\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F + J^2 - 3\right) - (\mu_1 + 2\mu_2)\ln J + \frac{\lambda}{2}(J - 1)^2 ,\]

with $\mu = 10^5$ and $\lambda = 6\mu$. Linearised, its shear modulus is $\mu_1 + \mu_2 = 2\mu$ and its first Lamé constant $\lambda + 2\mu$ (Poisson's ratio 0.4 in plane strain, as in the paper).

Third medium (Wriggers, Schröder and Schwarz 2013). The MIPS energy (called ARAP2D in HyperFEM), which penalises the distortion of the elements but not their size, plus a barrier that grows without bound when the area of an element goes to zero:

\[\Psi_{tm} = \frac{\mu_{tm}}{2}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F}{J} - 2\right) + \frac{\kappa}{2p}(\ln J)^{2p} , \qquad \mu_{tm} = f_c\,\mu ,\quad \kappa = \mu_{tm} ,\quad p = 2 ,\quad f_c = 10^{-6} .\]

While the gap is open the third medium is a million times softer than the column; when the column presses it against a wall, $J \to 0$ and the barrier transmits the contact pressure.

Regularisation (Bluhm, Sigmund and Poulios 2021). A soft medium that is squeezed develops spurious zig-zag modes. Bluhm et al. add the energy $\tfrac{\beta}{2}\int_{\Omega_{tm}} \nabla\nabla\boldsymbol u \,\vdots\, \nabla\nabla\boldsymbol u$, which penalises the curvature of the deformation of the third medium. With linear triangles $\nabla\nabla\boldsymbol u = 0$ inside every element and the curvature sits on the facets, as the jump of the gradient: the skeleton term above (interior facets $\Gamma_{tm}$ of the third medium, facet length $h_F$, $\beta = 10^{-5}\mu$, $\sigma = 1$) is its discrete counterpart, as in interior-penalty methods.

Reactions. The piston force is reported as $f/f_{cr}$, with the Euler load of the double-clamped column in plane strain, $f_{cr} = \pi^2 E' I / (L/2)^2$, $I = d^3/12$, $E' = E/(1 - \nu^2)$ of the linearised solid (the normalisation of Horák's curves).

Setup

using HyperFEM
using Gridap
using HyperFEMKernels                                          # compiled backend (`backend=:compiled`)
using Gridap.CellData: get_array                               # cell-wise values of an integral
using GridapGmsh
using LinearAlgebra
using Printf

const FAST = get(ENV, "HYPERFEM_TUTORIALS_FAST", "0") == "1"   # reduced case for the test suite
outdir = joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "sims", "E06_ThirdMedium_contact")
setupfolder(outdir)                                            # VTK and CSV output (not versioned)

Geometry and mesh

Gmsh meshes of linear triangles with two (Piston_1.msh, FAST) or four (Piston_0.5.msh, full) elements across the column. Their physical groups name the two subdomains, domain (the column) and third (the third medium), and the boundary pieces used below.

meshfile = FAST ? "Piston_1.msh" : "Piston_0.5.msh"
geomodel = GmshDiscreteModel(joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "models", meshfile))
d, Lb = 0.1, 1.8                                               # thickness and length of the column

Constitutive models

The hybrid Newton (see Solver below) needs two tangents of the same energy: the consistent one, and a projected one, positive definite at every quadrature point (the eigenvalues of $\partial^2_{\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol F}\Psi$ clamped from below at $\delta$). HessianRegularization wraps a model and returns the projected tangent while the shared TangentSwitch is on; the energy and the stress are not changed.

μ = 1e5                                                        # Pa
fc = 1e-6                                                      # stiffness of the third medium relative to μ
hrs = 1e-6                                                     # projection threshold relative to the stiffness
switch = TangentSwitch()
solid = HessianRegularization(NonlinearMooneyRivlin2D(λ=6μ, μ1=μ, μ2=μ, α1=1.0, α2=1.0); δ=hrs * μ, switch=switch)
medium = HessianRegularization(ARAP2D(μ=fc * μ) + LogBarrier(κ=fc * μ, p=2); δ=hrs * fc * μ, switch=switch)
Ψs, ∂Ψs∂F, ∂Ψs∂FF = solid()
Ψm, ∂Ψm∂F, ∂Ψm∂FF = medium()
F, _, _ = get_Kinematics(Kinematics(Mechano, Solid))
Fu(u) = F ∘ (∇(u)')                                            # deformation gradient of a displacement field

Boundary conditions

The tags come from the mesh. Imposed_Y_fixed_X is the top of the column, glued to the piston; Imposed_Y_free_X is the top of the third medium, which follows the piston but slides along it.

ū = FAST ? 0.15 : 0.40                                         # final piston displacement: 1.5 d (FAST) or 4 d
D_bc = BoundaryConditions(
  BoundaryCondition("Fixed_XY", [0.0, 0.0]),
  BoundaryCondition("Fixed_Y", [0.0, 0.0]; mask=(false, true)),
  BoundaryCondition("Fixed_X", [0.0, 0.0]; mask=(true, false)),
  BoundaryCondition("Imposed_Y_free_X", [0.0, -ū], EvolutionFunctions.ramp(); mask=(false, true)),
  BoundaryCondition("Imposed_Y_fixed_X", [0.0, -ū], EvolutionFunctions.ramp()))

Finite element spaces

One displacement field over the whole box (column and third medium together), and one measure per subdomain.

order = 1
reffe = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, VectorValue{2,Float64}, order)
Ω = Triangulation(geomodel, tags=["domain", "third"])
Ωs = Triangulation(geomodel, tags=["domain"])
Ωm = Triangulation(geomodel, tags=["third"])
dΩ, dΩs, dΩm = Measure(Ω, 2order), Measure(Ωs, 2order), Measure(Ωm, 2order)
V = TestFESpace(Ω, reffe, D_bc, conformity=:H1)
U = TrialFESpace(V, D_bc, 0.0)
uh = FEFunction(U, zeros(num_free_dofs(U)))
println("free dofs: ", num_free_dofs(V))

Weak form

The skeleton of the third medium: its interior facets, where jump(∇(u)) is the jump of the gradient between the two triangles of a facet. $h_F$ is computed as the integral of 1 over each facet.

Γ = SkeletonTriangulation(geomodel, tags=["third"])
dΓ = Measure(Γ, 2order)
hF = CellField(get_array(∫(1)dΓ), Γ)
β, σ = 1e-5 * μ, 1.0
cJ = (σ * β) / hF
qJ(a, b) = jump(∇(a)) ⊙ jump(∇(b))

res(Λ) = (u, v) -> ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ (∂Ψs∂F ∘ Fu(u)))dΩs +
                   ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ (∂Ψm∂F ∘ Fu(u)))dΩm +
                   ∫(cJ * qJ(u, v))dΓ
jac(Λ) = (u, du, v) -> ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ ((∂Ψs∂FF ∘ Fu(u)) ⋅² ∇(du)'))dΩs +
                       ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ ((∂Ψm∂FF ∘ Fu(u)) ⋅² ∇(du)'))dΩm +
                       ∫(cJ * qJ(du, v))dΓ

The energy $\Pi$, used by the line search below. Its first variation is the residual above.

en(Λ) = u -> ∫(Ψs ∘ Fu(u))dΩs + ∫(Ψm ∘ Fu(u))dΩm + ∫(0.5 * cJ * qJ(u, u))dΓ

Solver and computational model

Three ingredients make Newton robust here.

  1. Injectivity. The barrier is infinite at $J = 0$ and the energies are not defined for $J \le 0$: a full Newton step that inverts one triangle of the third medium is useless. InjectivityLS computes the largest step $\alpha$ that keeps $\det(\boldsymbol F + \alpha\nabla_0\delta\boldsymbol u) \ge J_{min}$ at every quadrature point of injectivity (a quadratic equation per point in 2D, solved exactly) and starts the line search from $0.999999\,\alpha$.
  2. Armijo on the energy. From there the step is halved until the energy decreases enough, $\Pi(\boldsymbol u + \alpha\delta\boldsymbol u) \le \Pi(\boldsymbol u) + c_1\alpha\,\boldsymbol R\cdot\delta\boldsymbol u$ (armijo=true, the energy given with energy=).
  3. Hybrid Newton. After the buckling the consistent tangent is indefinite and its Newton direction may not be a descent direction, so the line search fails. HybridTangent then switches the TangentSwitch on and continues with the projected tangent, which always gives a descent direction; it goes back to the consistent tangent (quadratic convergence) once $\|\boldsymbol R\|$ has dropped by hybback. In projected mode the convergence test is absolute, $\|\boldsymbol R\| < 10^{-3} f_{cr}$.

With the increments used below the consistent tangent turns out to be enough: the solver log reports 0 % of the iterations in projected mode, in FAST and in the full run. The hybrid tangent is the safety net for larger increments; with the growing increments of Going further it takes over in about a fifth of the iterations.

With backend=:compiled (tutorial 01) the residual, the tangent, the energy of the line search and the injectivity step are all evaluated by compiled kernels, which matters most for the line search: it evaluates the energy several times per Newton iteration.

μl, λl = 2μ, 6μ + 2μ                                           # linearised solid
Eps = 4μl * (λl + μl) / (λl + 2μl)                             # plane-strain modulus E/(1 - ν²)
fcr = π^2 * Eps * (d^3 / 12) / (0.5Lb)^2                       # Euler load of the double-clamped column

linesearch = InjectivityLS(jmin=1e-6, armijo=true, β=1 - 1e-6, ρ=0.5, maxiter=20, αmin=1e-16)
tangent = HybridTangent(hybback=1e-2, atol_projected=1e-3 * fcr)

Newton stops at $\|\boldsymbol R\| < 10^{-6}$ (about $10^{-9} f_{cr}$): every increment ends at an equilibrium, so the results (and the regression values below) do not depend on where the iterations happen to stop.

nls = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=500, atol=1e-6, rtol=1e-10, verbose=1, linesearch=linesearch, tangent=tangent)
comp_model = StaticNonlinearModel(res, jac, U, V, D_bc; nls=nls, xh=uh, injectivity=dΩ, energy=en, tangent_switch=switch,
                                backend=:compiled)

Post-processing

Reaction forces from the residual. At a converged state the residual vanishes on the free dofs, and on the Dirichlet dofs it is the reaction. Testing the residual with a function $\boldsymbol\varphi$ that is $\boldsymbol e_y$ on the nodes of a boundary and zero elsewhere gives the vertical force on that boundary, $f = \boldsymbol R(\boldsymbol u)\cdot\boldsymbol\varphi$. Such a $\boldsymbol\varphi$ is a finite element function whose free values are zero and whose Dirichlet values on the chosen tags are $(0, 1)$.

function unit_y_on(tags)
  Vr = TestFESpace(Ω, reffe, conformity=:H1, dirichlet_tags=tags, dirichlet_masks=[(false, true) for _ in tags])
  FEFunction(TrialFESpace(Vr, [VectorValue(0.0, 1.0) for _ in tags]), zeros(num_free_dofs(Vr)))
end
φpiston = unit_y_on(["Imposed_Y_fixed_X", "Imposed_Y_free_X"])
φclamp = unit_y_on(["Fixed_XY"])

After every converged increment we store the piston displacement and both reactions (the piston pushes down, so its force is $-\boldsymbol R\cdot\boldsymbol\varphi$) and write the displacement and $J$ every few increments.

uy_d, fpiston, fclamp = Float64[], Float64[], Float64[]
vtkevery = FAST ? 5 : 25
function record(post)
  Λ = post.Λ[post.iter]
  push!(uy_d, Λ * ū / d)
  push!(fpiston, -sum(res(Λ)(uh, φpiston)) / fcr)
  push!(fclamp, sum(res(Λ)(uh, φclamp)) / fcr)
  if post.iter % vtkevery == 0
    pvd = get_pvd(post)
    pvd[Λ] = createvtk(Ω, joinpath(outdir, "step_$(post.iter).vtu"), cellfields=["u" => uh, "J" => det ∘ Fu(uh)])
  end
end
post = PostProcessor(comp_model, record; is_vtk=true, filepath=outdir)

Solve

FAST: 15 equal increments up to $1.5\,d$, just past the buckling of the column. Full: 400 increments up to $4\,d$, through the first contact and the first snap; a bisected increment is allowed to grow back by 1.5 after an increment that converged in at most 10 Newton iterations. ProjectDirichlet=true predicts every increment from the linearised effect of the new piston position (tutorial 02), scaled down if needed so that no element is inverted.

stepping = FAST ? LoadStepping(nsteps=15, maxbisec=5) : LoadStepping(nsteps=400, maxbisec=5, grow=1.5)
nbisect = Ref(0)
t_solve = @elapsed x, flag = solve!(comp_model; stepping=stepping, ProjectDirichlet=true, post=post,
                                    on_bisection=(Λ, ΔΛ) -> nbisect[] += 1)   # also closes the .pvd
@printf("solve: %.1f s, %d increments, %d bisections\n", t_solve, length(uy_d), nbisect[])

open(joinpath(outdir, "force_displacement.csv"), "w") do io
  println(io, "uy_over_d,f_piston_over_fcr,f_clamp_over_fcr")
  for (a, b, c) in zip(uy_d, fpiston, fclamp)
    println(io, a, ",", b, ",", c)
  end
end

Checks

Equilibrium. The third medium is so soft that the piston force must reach the bottom clamp through the column: both reactions agree within 1 % at every increment.

@assert flag == 1 "the load stepping did not reach Λ = 1"
@assert all(abs.(fpiston .- fclamp) .< 1e-2 .* abs.(fpiston)) "piston and clamp reactions are not in equilibrium"

Buckling. Without buckling the force would grow linearly with the shortening of the column (by a factor of 3 from $0.5\,d$ to $1.5\,d$); a buckled column carries an almost constant force, the plateau of the curve.

i05, i15 = argmin(abs.(uy_d .- 0.5)), argmin(abs.(uy_d .- 1.5))
@assert fpiston[i15] < 1.1 * fpiston[i05] "the column did not buckle before u_y = 0.5 d"

Comparison with the paper (printed, not asserted). Horák's curve is computed with quadratic elements. Linear triangles are too stiff in bending when there are few of them across the column: with two (FAST mesh) the buckling plateau comes out about 60 % above the paper's; with four (full mesh) about 7 % above, and the snap ($3.70\,f_{cr}$ at $2.96\,d$, against $3.43\,f_{cr}$ at $3.0\,d$ in the paper) is in the right place.

function horak_curve(file, key)
  s = read(file, String)
  block = match(Regex("\"$key\": \\[(.*?)\\]\\]"), s).captures[1]
  pts = [parse.(Float64, (m[1], m[2])) for m in eachmatch(r"\[(-?[0-9.eE+-]+), (-?[0-9.eE+-]+)\]", block * "]")]
  first.(pts), last.(pts)
end
hx, hy = horak_curve(joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "reference", "horak2026_piston_f_vs_uy.json"), "piston")
interp(xs, ys, x) = (i = clamp(searchsortedlast(xs, x), 1, length(xs) - 1); ys[i] + (ys[i+1] - ys[i]) * (x - xs[i]) / (xs[i+1] - xs[i]))
for x in (FAST ? (0.5, 1.0, 1.5) : (0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0))
  i = argmin(abs.(uy_d .- x))
  @printf("u_y/d = %.3f   f/f_cr = %.4f   (Horák %.4f)\n", uy_d[i], fpiston[i], interp(hx, hy, uy_d[i]))
end
imax = argmax(fpiston)
@printf("maximum force f/f_cr = %.4f at u_y/d = %.3f\n", fpiston[imax], uy_d[imax])
@printf("‖u‖ = %.10f   f/f_cr at the end = %.10f\n", norm(get_free_dof_values(uh)), fpiston[end])

Regression (FAST; HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8, Julia 1.13.0, macOS arm64), and, in the full run, the first snap: the column touches the walls, the force rises steeply and then drops when the column snaps into a new shape: the maximum of the force is reached before $3.4\,d$, and the force at $4\,d$ is below it.

if FAST
  @assert length(uy_d) == 15 && nbisect[] == 0 "an increment was bisected"
  @assert isapprox(norm(get_free_dof_values(uh)), 3.8344902564, rtol=1e-5) "regression: ‖u‖"
  @assert isapprox(fpiston[end], 1.7420291165, rtol=1e-5) "regression: piston force at 1.5 d"
else
  @assert uy_d[imax] < 3.4 && fpiston[end] < fpiston[imax] "no snap before u_y = 3.4 d"
end

Going further

  • Run the whole benchmark, up to $15\,d$: ū = 1.5 and LoadStepping(nsteps=1000, maxbisec=5, grow=1.5) (several minutes); the column folds twice more against the walls. Compare with the full curve in data/reference/horak2026_piston_f_vs_uy.json (keys piston and clamp).
  • Let the increments grow beyond the initial one: LoadStepping(nsteps=400, maxbisec=5, grow=1.5, ΔΛmax=0.01, neasy=2) reaches $4\,d$ in about 100 increments. On Piston_1.msh it gives the same curve as 400 fixed increments (maximum $5.78\,f_{cr}$ at $3.32\,d$), and the hybrid Newton is now at work: 21 % of the Newton iterations use the projected tangent (5 switches). mode_history(nls) lists the mode of every iteration of the last Newton solve.
  • Remove the barrier (ARAP2D(μ=fc * μ) alone) or the skeleton term, and compare the force curve and the field $J$ of the third medium under the column.
  • Bluhm et al. also weight the regularisation with a function $w(\boldsymbol F)$ that grows with compression and distortion; the residual is then no longer the derivative of an energy, and the line search must use InjectivityLS(armijo=false) (backtracking on $|\boldsymbol R\cdot\delta\boldsymbol u|$) instead of Armijo.
  • ConsistentTangent() instead of HybridTangent: a failed line search now fails the increment, and the driver has to bisect it; compare the number of bisections after the buckling.
