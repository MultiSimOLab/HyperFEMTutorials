Tutorial 05: Anisotropic electro-mechanics

Source · Notebook · nbviewer

The dielectric elastomer plate of tutorial 04, now reinforced by a family of stiff fibres lying in the plane of the plate at 45° between its width and its length. The fibres resist the in-plane elongation of the active layer along their direction, so the plate no longer deforms symmetrically: besides bending, it shears and twists. The constitutive model is built by adding three models, and the fibre direction enters the weak form as an extra field.

What you will learn

  • composing constitutive models with + (isotropic matrix, fibres, dielectric);
  • passing a field of material directions to an anisotropic model (the extra arguments vars...);
  • the field-by-field helpers residual(model, Mechano, ...), residual(model, Electro, ...) and the coupling tangent jacobian(model, ElectroMechano, ...), which accept those extra arguments;
  • detecting the effect of anisotropy through a broken symmetry.

Measured on a Mac M5 Max (Julia 1.13.0, 4 threads, 4 BLAS threads; HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8; compiled backend), whole run including compilation: FAST 45 s, full 46 s (20 increments, 109 Newton iterations, 16 s in solve! including its compilation); 1 528 free dofs. One assembly of the tangent: 0.7 ms compiled, 13 ms with Gridap's lazy evaluation.

Problem statement

Geometry, boundary conditions and weak form are those of tutorial 04. The free energy adds to the neo-Hookean and ideal-dielectric energies the Holzapfel–Gasser–Ogden energy of one family of fibres with unit direction $\boldsymbol N$ in the reference configuration,

\[\Psi = \Psi_{\mathrm{NH}}(\boldsymbol F) + \frac{c_1}{4 c_2}\left(e^{c_2 (I_4 - 1)^2} - 1\right) - \frac{\varepsilon}{2J}\,|\boldsymbol H \boldsymbol E_0|^2 , \qquad I_4 = \boldsymbol F\boldsymbol N \cdot \boldsymbol F\boldsymbol N ,\]

with $c_1 = 10$, $c_2 = 20$ (in units of $\mu = 1$) and $\boldsymbol N = (1, 0, 1)/\sqrt 2$: the fibres lie in the plane of the plate, at 45° between the width $x$ and the length $z$. $I_4$ is the squared stretch of the fibres. (This form of the energy acts in compression too: fibres that can only be pulled would need a switch on $I_4 > 1$.)

Reflecting the plate about its mid-plane $x = 4$ maps $\boldsymbol N$ to $(-1, 0, 1)/\sqrt 2$, a different material: the symmetry of tutorial 04 is lost.

Setup

using HyperFEM
using Gridap
using HyperFEMKernels                                          # compiled backend (`backend=:compiled`)
using LinearAlgebra: norm

const FAST = get(ENV, "HYPERFEM_TUTORIALS_FAST", "0") == "1"   # reduced case for the test suite
outdir = joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "sims", "E05_ElectroMechanics_anisotropic")
setupfolder(outdir)                                            # VTK output (not versioned)

Geometry and mesh

model = GmshDiscreteModel(joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "models", "test_static_EM.msh"))
labels = get_face_labeling(model)
add_tag_from_vertex_filter!(labels, "tip", model, x -> abs(x[3] - 40.0) < 1e-8)

Constitutive model

+ composes models: an isotropic and an anisotropic mechanical model give an anisotropic mechanical model, and a mechanical and an electrical model give an ElectroMechModel. The derivatives of the composition are the sums of the derivatives; they now take the fibre direction as a third argument, $(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol E_0, \boldsymbol N)$.

physmodel = NeoHookean3D(λ=10.0, μ=1.0) + HGO_1Fiber(c1=10.0, c2=20.0) + IdealDielectric(ε=1.0)
k = (Kinematics(Mechano, Solid), Kinematics(Electro, Solid))

Boundary conditions

The full case stops at 0.25 instead of the 0.3 of tutorial 04: with the fibres, Newton's iterations diverge at about 0.27 (the iterations of the previous increments already converge erratically) and, in HyperFEM 0.3.0, the run ends with an uncaught SingularException of the LU factorisation instead of a bisection.

V0 = FAST ? 0.1 : 0.25                                         # voltage between the electrodes
nsteps = FAST ? 5 : 20
Du = BoundaryConditions(BoundaryCondition("fixedup", [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]))
Dφ = BoundaryConditions(BoundaryCondition("midsuf", 0.0, Λ -> Λ), BoundaryCondition("topsuf", V0, Λ -> Λ))
D_bc = MultiFieldBC([Du, Dφ])

Finite element spaces

order = 1
reffeu = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, VectorValue{3,Float64}, order)
reffeφ = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, Float64, order)
Vu = TestFESpace(model, reffeu, D_bc[1], conformity=:H1)
Vφ = TestFESpace(model, reffeφ, D_bc[2], conformity=:H1)
Uu = TrialFESpace(Vu, D_bc[1], 0.0)
Uφ = TrialFESpace(Vφ, D_bc[2], 0.0)
V = MultiFieldFESpace([Vu, Vφ])
U = MultiFieldFESpace([Uu, Uφ])
Ω = Triangulation(model)
dΩ = Measure(Ω, 2 * order)
println("free dofs: ", num_free_dofs(U), " (u: ", num_free_dofs(Uu), ", φ: ", num_free_dofs(Uφ), ")")

Fibre field

The direction is a finite element field, so it could vary in space (read from a file, or computed, as the fibres of tutorial 03). Here it is uniform. We give it unit length; HGO_1Fiber also normalises it at every quadrature point, so only its direction matters.

VN = FESpace(model, reffeu)                                    # no boundary conditions
Nh = interpolate_everywhere(VectorValue(1.0, 0.0, 1.0) / sqrt(2.0), VN)

Weak form

The forms of tutorial 04 with Nh appended to the arguments of every constitutive function. The helpers do it for us: every one of them takes the extra arguments of the model after the load factor $\Lambda$. The residual is the sum of the mechanical and electrical equations, and the tangent the sum of the two diagonal blocks and the two coupling blocks (ElectroMechano).

res(Λ) = ((u, φ), (v, vφ)) ->
  residual(physmodel, Mechano, k, (u, φ), v, dΩ, Λ, Nh) +
  residual(physmodel, Electro, k, (u, φ), vφ, dΩ, Λ, Nh)

jac(Λ) = ((u, φ), (du, dφ), (v, vφ)) ->
  jacobian(physmodel, Mechano, k, (u, φ), du, v, dΩ, Λ, Nh) +
  jacobian(physmodel, Electro, k, (u, φ), dφ, vφ, dΩ, Λ, Nh) +
  jacobian(physmodel, ElectroMechano, k, (u, φ), (du, dφ), (v, vφ), dΩ, Λ, Nh)

Solver and computational model

The compiled backend (tutorial 01) also accepts the fibre field: Nh is read at the quadrature points like the state.

nls = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=20, atol=1e-10, rtol=1e-8, verbose=true)
comp_model = StaticNonlinearModel(res, jac, U, V, D_bc; nls=nls, backend=:compiled)
xh = get_state(comp_model)
uh, φh = xh

Post-processing

The mean displacement of the free end at every increment, and the VTK files (with the fibre field).

Γtip = BoundaryTriangulation(model, tags="tip")
dΓtip = Measure(Γtip, 2 * order)
Atip = sum(∫(1.0)dΓtip)
voltage, tip_u = Float64[], VectorValue{3,Float64}[]
function record(post)
  Λ = post.Λ[post.iter]
  push!(voltage, Λ * V0)
  push!(tip_u, sum(∫(uh)dΓtip) / Atip)
  pvd = get_pvd(post)
  pvd[Λ] = createvtk(Ω, joinpath(outdir, "step_$(post.iter).vtu"), cellfields=["u" => uh, "φ" => φh, "N" => Nh])
end
post = PostProcessor(comp_model, record; is_vtk=true, filepath=outdir)

Solve

x, flag = solve!(comp_model; stepping=LoadStepping(nsteps=nsteps, maxbisec=5), ProjectDirichlet=true, post=post)

for (vo, w) in zip(voltage, tip_u)
  println("V = $(round(vo, digits=4))   mean tip displacement (u_x, u_y, u_z) = $(round.(Tuple(w), sigdigits=6))")
end

Checks

Broken symmetry. In tutorial 04 the problem is symmetric about the plane $x = 4$: the displacement at a point and at its mirror image $(8 - x, y, z)$ are mirror images of each other, $u_x$ with the opposite sign and $u_y$, $u_z$ equal. We measure the largest departure from that, at a set of points of the plate, relative to the deflection of the free end. It is zero to round-off in tutorial 04; the fibres make it a visible fraction of the deflection. (The mean of $u_x$ would also do, but it changes sign along the load path.)

mirror(p) = Point(8.0 - p[1], p[2], p[3])
pts = [Point(x, y, z) for x in (0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3.5) for y in (0.2, 0.6) for z in (10.0, 20.0, 30.0, 39.5)]
asym = maximum(norm(VectorValue(a[1] + b[1], a[2] - b[2], a[3] - b[3])) for (a, b) in zip(uh(pts), uh(mirror.(pts))))
tip_uy = tip_u[end][2]
println("asymmetry max|u(p) - mirrored u(p')| = $(asym)   tip u = $(tip_u[end])")
@assert flag == 1 "the load stepping did not reach Λ = 1"
@assert tip_uy < 0 "the plate does not bend towards -y"
@assert asym > 1e-2 * abs(tip_uy) "the fibres do not break the symmetry about x = 4"

Regression (HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8, Julia 1.13.0, macOS arm64).

nu = norm(get_free_dof_values(uh))

Going further

  • Turn the fibres: along the length, VectorValue(0.0, 0.0, 1.0), or across the width, VectorValue(1.0, 0.0, 0.0), they keep the symmetry about $x = 4$ (the mean of $u_x$ vanishes again); compare the deflections. A fibre field that varies in space only needs a function in interpolate_everywhere.
  • Add a second family with another HGO_1Fiber and another field (the arguments then follow the order of the models in the sum), or use HGO_4Fibers as in tutorial 03.
  • Compare with tutorial 04: the fibres reduce the deflection at the same voltage.
