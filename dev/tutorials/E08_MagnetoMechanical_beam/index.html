Tutorial 08: Magneto-mechanical hMRE beam

Source · Notebook · nbviewer

A slender beam of a hard-magnetic magnetorheological elastomer (hMRE: hard magnetic particles in an ultra-soft silicone matrix) is magnetised along its length and clamped at one end. A transverse magnetic field exerts a torque on the magnetisation, which tries to align with the field: the beam bends. This is the simplified model of Perez-Garcia, Ortigosa, Martínez-Frutos and Garcia-Gonzalez (Adv. Mater. 2025): the applied field acts on the solid only, and the field created by the beam itself and the surrounding air are ignored. Tutorial 09 solves the same beam with the magnetic field in the surrounding vacuum.

What you will learn

  • two-dimensional (plane strain) models: NonlinearMooneyRivlin2D + TransverseIsotropy2D;
  • a domain made of one subdomain of a larger mesh (Triangulation(model, tags=...));
  • a load written by hand and evaluated at the previous converged state: the state pair xh/xh⁻ of StaticNonlinearModel;
  • evaluating a finite element function at a point.

Measured on a Mac M5 Max (Julia 1.13.0, 4 threads, 4 BLAS threads; HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8; compiled backend), whole run including compilation: 25 s (FAST and full are the same case), of which 5 s in solve! (20 increments, 137 Newton iterations); 1 232 free dofs.

Problem statement

The beam $\Omega = (0, L)\times(-t/2, t/2)$, with $L = 50$ mm and $t = 2$ mm, is in plane strain. It is clamped on the first 2.5 mm of its lower face and cannot move along $x$ at its left end. Its residual magnetisation has the direction $\boldsymbol N = (1, 0)$ in the reference configuration and is carried by the rotation $\boldsymbol R = \boldsymbol F\boldsymbol U^{-1}$ of the polar decomposition ($\boldsymbol U^2 = \boldsymbol F^T\boldsymbol F$). The applied field $\boldsymbol B_a = \Lambda\,(0, -B_a)$ is uniform and grows with the load factor $\Lambda$. The magnetic (Zeeman) energy and its stress, with $\boldsymbol U^{-1}$ taken as fixed when differentiating, are

\[\Psi_{\mathrm{mag}} = -\frac{\alpha_r}{\mu_0}\, \boldsymbol B_a \cdot \boldsymbol R \boldsymbol N , \qquad \boldsymbol P_{\mathrm{mag}} = -\frac{\alpha_r}{\mu_0}\, \boldsymbol B_a \otimes \boldsymbol U^{-1}\boldsymbol N ,\]

with $\mu_0 = 4\pi\cdot 10^{-7}$ H/m, the remanent magnetisation $\alpha_r = 10^{-2}/\mu_0 \approx 7.96$ kA/m (10 mT divided by $\mu_0$) and $B_a = 8\cdot 10^{-8}$, the values of the paper's script. In that script, and here, $B_a$ is $\mu_0$ times the applied flux density: the applied field is $B_a/\mu_0 \approx 64$ mT, the energy is the usual $-\boldsymbol M\cdot\boldsymbol B$ with $\boldsymbol M = \alpha_r \boldsymbol R\boldsymbol N$, and the magnetic stress scale $\alpha_r B_a/\mu_0$ is about 0.5 kPa. The matrix is an ultra-soft silicone with a slight residual anisotropy along $\boldsymbol N$, fitted in the paper: the sum of a nonlinear Mooney–Rivlin energy and a transversely isotropic one (in Pa),

\[\begin{aligned} \Psi_{\mathrm{MR}} &= \frac{\mu_1}{2\alpha_1 3^{\alpha_1-1}}(\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F + 1)^{\alpha_1} + \frac{\mu_2}{2\alpha_2 3^{\alpha_2-1}}(\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F + J^2)^{\alpha_2} - (\mu_1 + 2\mu_2)\ln J + \frac{\lambda}{2}(J-1)^2 + \text{const}, \\ \Psi_{\mathrm{TI}} &= \frac{\mu}{2\beta_1}(I_4^{\beta_1} - 1) + \frac{\mu}{2\beta_2}(I_5^{\beta_2} - 1) - \mu\ln J , \qquad I_4 = |\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol N|^2 ,\; I_5 = |\boldsymbol H\boldsymbol N|^2 , \end{aligned}\]

(in the 2D models $\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F + 1$ and $\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F + J^2$ are the first two invariants of the plane-strain deformation). The weak form is

\[\int_\Omega \nabla_0\boldsymbol v : \left(\partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi_{\mathrm{MR}} + \partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi_{\mathrm{TI}} + \boldsymbol P_{\mathrm{mag}}\right) \mathrm dA = 0 \quad \forall \boldsymbol v .\]

The magnetic stress is evaluated at the displacement of the previous converged increment, $\boldsymbol u^{-}$, so that within an increment it is a given load and the tangent is that of the elastic energy alone. This explicit treatment of the rotation makes the result depend (slightly) on the size of the increments.

Setup

using HyperFEM
using Gridap
using HyperFEMKernels                                          # compiled backend (`backend=:compiled`)
using LinearAlgebra: norm

const FAST = get(ENV, "HYPERFEM_TUTORIALS_FAST", "0") == "1"   # the test suite runs the same case (see *Solve*)
outdir = joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "sims", "E08_MagnetoMechanical_beam")
setupfolder(outdir)                                            # VTK output (not versioned)

Geometry and mesh

The mesh of the paper: the beam, a region below it meant for a permanent magnet in other variants of the problem, and the surrounding air, in linear triangles. Here only the beam (physical group Beam) is used; tutorial 09 uses the rest, all of it as vacuum.

model = GmshDiscreteModel(joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "models", "Short_magnet.msh"))
L = 0.05                                                       # m

Constitutive model

Parameters fitted in the paper (Pa); + composes the isotropic and the anisotropic models.

params = [10.0, 12480.64495232286, 1.0, 2.0, 5195.545287237134, 0.2602717127043121, 2.0]
modelmech = NonlinearMooneyRivlin2D(λ=(params[1] + params[2]) * 1e2, μ1=params[1] * 1e3, μ2=params[2],
                                    α1=params[3], α2=params[4]) +
            TransverseIsotropy2D(μ=params[5], α1=params[6], α2=params[7])
km = Kinematics(Mechano, Solid)
F, _, _ = get_Kinematics(km)

Magnetic constants. They are read inside Pmag below, a function evaluated at every quadrature point, so they are declared const: a non-constant global read there would make every evaluation dynamic (and slow).

const μ0 = 4π * 1e-7                                           # H/m
const αr = 10e-3 / μ0                                          # A/m, remanent magnetisation
Banorm = 80.0e-9                                               # μ0 × applied flux density: Banorm/μ0 ≈ 64 mT

Boundary conditions

Fixed_Solid (the clamped part of the lower face) in both components; Fixed_Solid_x (the left end) along $x$ only.

Du = BoundaryConditions(
  BoundaryCondition("Fixed_Solid", [0.0, 0.0]),
  BoundaryCondition("Fixed_Solid_x", [0.0, 0.0]; mask=(true, false)))

Finite element spaces

Spaces on the beam only. Two FE functions hold the current state $\boldsymbol u$ and the state of the previous converged increment $\boldsymbol u^{-}$; the computational model updates both (xh, xh⁻).

order = 1
reffeu = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, VectorValue{2,Float64}, order)
Ω = Triangulation(model, tags=["Beam"])
dΩ = Measure(Ω, 2 * order)
V = TestFESpace(Ω, reffeu, Du, conformity=:H1)
U = TrialFESpace(V, Du)
U⁻ = TrialFESpace(V, Du)
uh = FEFunction(U, zeros(num_free_dofs(U)))
uh⁻ = FEFunction(U⁻, zeros(num_free_dofs(U⁻)))
println("free dofs: ", num_free_dofs(U), "   triangles: ", num_cells(Ω))

Magnetisation direction and applied field, as finite element fields on the beam.

VN = TestFESpace(Ω, reffeu)
Nh = interpolate_everywhere(x -> VectorValue(1.0, 0.0), VN)
Bah = interpolate_everywhere(VectorValue(0.0, -Banorm), VN)

Weak form

$\boldsymbol U^{-1} = (\boldsymbol F^T\boldsymbol F)^{-1/2}$ at a point, with a small shift that keeps the square root well defined, and the magnetic stress. They are plain Julia functions of tensors, composed with FE fields like the derivatives of the constitutive models.

function invU(F; ε=1e-12)
  S = get_array(F)' * get_array(F) + ε * get_array(I2D)
  TensorValue(inv(sqrt(S)))
end
Pmag(F, N, Ba) = (-αr / μ0) * (Ba ⊗ (invU(F) * N))

The elastic terms come from the helpers (Nh is the extra argument of the anisotropic model); the magnetic term is written by hand, with $\boldsymbol F$ of uh⁻ and multiplied by the load factor. It does not depend on the unknown $\boldsymbol u$, so it does not enter the tangent.

res(Λ) = (u, v) -> residual(modelmech, km, u, v, dΩ, Λ, Nh) +
                   Λ * ∫((Pmag ∘ (F ∘ ∇(uh⁻)', Nh, Bah)) ⊙ ∇(v)')dΩ
jac(Λ) = (u, du, v) -> jacobian(modelmech, km, u, du, v, dΩ, Λ, Nh)

Solver and computational model

The paper's script stops Newton at a 1 % relative residual (rtol=1e-2), on the grounds that the magnetic load is lagged by one increment anyway. Here every increment is solved to equilibrium (rtol=1e-8): the result then depends on the increments only, not on where the iterations happened to stop. With backend=:compiled (tutorial 01) the hand-written magnetic term, invU included, is compiled with the rest of the form; at every increment it is only rebound to the new uh⁻ and $\Lambda$.

nls = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=20, atol=1e-10, rtol=1e-8, verbose=true)
comp_model = StaticNonlinearModel(res, jac, U, V, Du; nls=nls, xh=uh, xh⁻=uh⁻, backend=:compiled)

Post-processing

After every increment: the displacement of the middle of the free end, $(L, 0)$, and the VTK files.

tip = Point(L, 0.0)
field, tip_u = Float64[], VectorValue{2,Float64}[]
function record(post)
  Λ = post.Λ[post.iter]
  push!(field, Λ * Banorm / μ0)                                # applied flux density, T
  push!(tip_u, uh(tip))
  pvd = get_pvd(post)
  pvd[Λ] = createvtk(Ω, joinpath(outdir, "step_$(post.iter).vtu"), cellfields=["u" => uh])
end
post = PostProcessor(comp_model, record; is_vtk=true, filepath=outdir)

Solve

Twenty equal increments, as in the paper; one bisection at most. The beam is so soft that the first increments need many Newton iterations, and with five increments the first one diverges. The whole run takes seconds, so the FAST mode of the test suite runs this same case.

x, flag = solve!(comp_model; stepping=LoadStepping(nsteps=20, maxbisec=1), post=post)

for (b, w) in zip(field, tip_u)
  println("B = $(round(1e3b, sigdigits=4)) mT   tip displacement = ($(round(w[1]*1e3, sigdigits=6)), $(round(w[2]*1e3, sigdigits=6))) mm")
end

Checks

Physics. The beam bends towards the field ($-y$), more at every increment, and its free end stays at a distance from the clamp no larger than the length of the beam (a soft but not extensible bending).

tip_end = tip_u[end]
println("tip displacement: ", tip_end, "   ‖u‖ = ", norm(get_free_dof_values(uh)))
@assert flag == 1 "the load stepping did not reach Λ = 1"
@assert all(diff([w[2] for w in tip_u]) .< 0) && tip_end[2] < 0 "the beam does not bend towards the field"
@assert norm(VectorValue(L, 0.0) + tip_end) < 1.02 * L "the free end moved too far"

Reference. The norm of the displacement against the script of the paper, Adv_Mater_Ultra_Soft_Magnet/src/hMRE_2D.jl (HyperFEM 0.3.0, Gridap 0.20.8, Julia 1.13.0, macOS arm64), which stops Newton at a 1 % relative residual: the two agree to 4e-8, so the paper's looser tolerance did not change its result. The tip displacement is a regression value of this script (same versions, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6).

nu = norm(get_free_dof_values(uh))
nu_ref = 0.7146295685790958                                    # m, paper's script
@assert isapprox(nu, nu_ref; rtol=1e-6) "‖u‖ = $nu differs from the reference $nu_ref"
tip_ref = [-0.034300973255553015, -0.04289853199660556]        # m
@assert isapprox(collect(Tuple(tip_end)), tip_ref; rtol=1e-5) "tip displacement $tip_end differs from the reference $tip_ref"

Going further

  • Run with LoadStepping(nsteps=40, maxbisec=1): the change of the tip displacement measures the error of the lagged magnetic load.
  • Reverse the field, or turn it: $\boldsymbol B_a$ along $\boldsymbol N$ exerts no torque, and the beam only stretches or shortens along its axis.
  • Tutorial 09 computes the magnetic field in the beam and in the surrounding vacuum, driven by a magnetic potential difference across the box, and solves magnetics and mechanics one after the other (StaggeredModel).
  • Reference: C. Perez-Garcia, R. Ortigosa, J. Martínez-Frutos, D. Garcia-Gonzalez, Topology and material optimization in ultra-soft magnetoactive structures: making advantage of residual anisotropies, Adv. Mater. (2025) e18489, doi:10.1002/adma.202518489.
