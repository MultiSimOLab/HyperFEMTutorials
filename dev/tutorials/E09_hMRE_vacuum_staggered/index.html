Tutorial 09: hMRE beam in vacuum: staggered solution

Source · Notebook · nbviewer

A thin beam of hard-magnetic soft elastomer (hMRE: an ultra-soft silicone filled with magnetised particles) bends when an external magnetic field is applied across its remanent magnetisation. Here the beam is surrounded by an explicit layer of vacuum (air) that carries the magnetic field and deforms with the beam, so that the field, the Maxwell stress on the beam and the beam itself are computed together. The coupled problem is split into three problems solved one after another at every load step (a staggered, or partitioned, solution): this is how the simulations of Perez-Garcia et al. (Adv. Mater. 2025) were run.

What you will learn

  • magneto-mechanical models: HardMagnetic2D inside a MagnetoMechModel, IdealMagnetic2D for the vacuum, and a regularised fictitious material (HessianRegularization) for the motion of the vacuum mesh;
  • finite element spaces on parts of a mesh (solid, vacuum, their interface) and a field on an interface;
  • StaggeredModel and StaggeredStepping: three StaticNonlinearModels solved in turn, with the states of the other fields read between their previous and current values (StageBlend);
  • prescribing the state of another problem on a boundary, BoundaryCondition(tag, Coupling(...));
  • moving nodal values between meshes with Transfer/transfer! (the Maxwell traction of the vacuum on the beam);
  • the record of a staggered solve, get_log.

Measured on a Mac M5 Max (Julia 1.13.0, 4 threads, 4 BLAS threads; HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8; compiled backend), whole run including compilation: FAST 74 s, full 80 s (20 steps of 2 passes, 28 s in solve!, of which about 11 s compile the three problems at the first pass). 25 375 unknowns: 8 563 magnetic potential, 1 232 solid displacement, 15 580 vacuum displacement. One assembly of the tangent, compiled against Gridap: magnetic 3.9 ms / 27 ms, vacuum 6.0 ms / 29 ms.

Problem statement

The mesh is half of the problem ($x \ge 0$; $x = 0$ is a plane of symmetry): a beam $\Omega_s = (0, 50) \times (-1, 1)$ mm, clamped on its lower face for $x < 2.5$ mm, inside a box of vacuum $\Omega_v$ of 350 × 450 mm. The magnetic scalar potential $\varphi$ is prescribed on the bottom and top of the box, $\varphi = \Lambda\,\varphi_b$ and $\varphi = 0$, which applies a vertical field $\mathcal H_0 \approx \Lambda\,\varphi_b / 0.45$ A/m; the load factor $\Lambda$ goes from 0 to 1.

All the unknowns are written in the reference configuration: the displacement $\boldsymbol u$ (of the beam and, fictitiously, of the vacuum), $\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I + \nabla_0 \boldsymbol u$, $\boldsymbol H = \operatorname{cof} \boldsymbol F$, $J = \det \boldsymbol F$, and the Lagrangian magnetic field $\boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0 = -\nabla_0 \varphi$. In the vacuum the energy is that of free space mapped to the reference configuration,

\[\Psi_v(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0) = -\frac{\mu_0}{2J}\, \lvert \boldsymbol H \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0 \rvert^2 ,\]

and in the beam it adds the elastic energy and the energy of the remanent field $\boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_r = \alpha_r \boldsymbol N$ along the magnetisation direction $\boldsymbol N = (1, 0)$:

\[\Psi_s(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0, \boldsymbol N) = \Psi_{\mathrm{mec}}(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol N) - \frac{\mu_0}{2J}\,\lvert \boldsymbol H \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0 \rvert^2 + \frac{\mu_0 (1 + \chi_t)}{J}\,(\boldsymbol H \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0) \cdot (\boldsymbol H \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_r) .\]

Here $\chi_t = 0$ (the keyword χt of HardMagnetic2D defaults to the susceptibility χe = 0); the constructor's χr only enters a further term weighted by βmok, which is zero by default, so it is not set. The last term is the torque on the magnetised particles: a field across $\boldsymbol N$ rotates the beam. In the undeformed state the induction is $\boldsymbol{\mathcal B}_0 = -\partial\Psi_s/\partial\boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0 = \mu_0\,(\boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0 - (1+\chi_t)\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_r)$: with this sign convention the remanent magnetisation points along $-\boldsymbol N$, so an upward field gives a clockwise torque and the free end of the beam moves down.

The three problems, solved in this order at every load step, are:

  1. Magnetic potential on $\Omega_s \cup \Omega_v$ (Gauss's law for the magnetic induction $\boldsymbol{\mathcal B}_0 = -\partial \Psi / \partial \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0$) with the displacements fixed:

    \[-\int_{\Omega_s} \frac{\partial \Psi_s}{\partial \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0} \cdot \nabla_0 \delta\varphi \,\mathrm dV -\int_{\Omega_v} \frac{\partial \Psi_v}{\partial \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0} \cdot \nabla_0 \delta\varphi \,\mathrm dV = 0 .\]

  2. Mechanics of the beam with the magnetic field fixed, loaded on the interface $\Gamma$ by the Maxwell stress $\boldsymbol P_v = \partial \Psi_v / \partial \boldsymbol F$ of the vacuum:

    \[\int_{\Omega_s} \frac{\partial \Psi_s}{\partial \boldsymbol F} : \nabla_0 \delta\boldsymbol u \,\mathrm dV + \int_{\Gamma} \boldsymbol t_M \cdot \delta\boldsymbol u \,\mathrm dA = 0 ,\]

    where $\boldsymbol t_M$ reproduces at the nodes of $\Gamma$ the nodal forces $\int_{\Omega_v} \boldsymbol P_v : \nabla_0 \delta\boldsymbol u \,\mathrm dV$ of the vacuum (see Maxwell traction on the interface).
  3. Motion of the vacuum: a fictitious hyperelastic problem on $\Omega_v$ with $\boldsymbol u_v = \boldsymbol u_s$ on $\Gamma$, whose only purpose is to let the vacuum mesh follow the beam without inverting its elements.

Splitting the problem this way keeps three small, well-conditioned systems, at the price of a coupling that is only satisfied after enough passes; here every load step makes a fixed number of passes (see Solve).

Reference: C. Perez-Garcia, R. Ortigosa, J. Martínez-Frutos, D. Garcia-Gonzalez, Topology and material optimization in ultra-soft magnetoactive structures: making advantage of residual anisotropies, Adv. Mater. (2025), doi:10.1002/adma.202518489.

Setup

using HyperFEM
using Gridap, GridapGmsh

const FAST = get(ENV, "HYPERFEM_TUTORIALS_FAST", "0") == "1"   # reduced case for the test suite

Geometry and mesh

A Gmsh mesh of linear triangles (lengths in m) with physical groups for the beam (Beam), the vacuum (vacuumb, which includes a region kept for a permanent magnet in other variants of the problem), the interface between them (Interface) and the boundary conditions.

geomodel = GmshDiscreteModel(joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "models", "Short_magnet.msh"))

Constitutive models

SI units (m, Pa, A). The beam: a nonlinear Mooney–Rivlin matrix plus a transversely isotropic term along $\boldsymbol N$ (the residual anisotropy left by the magnetisation process), with the parameters of the paper for a Dowsil-based hMRE (shear moduli of about 10 kPa), and the hard-magnetic coupling with a remanent field $\mu_0 \mathcal H_r = 10$ mT. MagnetoMechModel adds the two; calling it returns the energy and its derivatives with respect to $\boldsymbol F$ and $\boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0$, functions of $(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0, \boldsymbol N)$.

μ0 = 4π * 1e-7                                                 # H/m
params = [10.0, 12480.64495232286, 1.0, 2.0, 5195.545287237134, 0.2602717127043121, 2.0]
model_mech = NonlinearMooneyRivlin2D(λ=(params[1] + params[2]) * 1e2, μ1=params[1] * 1e3, μ2=params[2], α1=params[3], α2=params[4]) +
             TransverseIsotropy2D(μ=params[5], α1=params[6], α2=params[7])
model_hard = HardMagnetic2D(μ0=μ0, αr=10e-3 / μ0, χe=0.0)
model_solid = MagnetoMechModel(magneto=model_hard, mechano=model_mech)

The vacuum: free space for the magnetic problem, and for its motion a soft compressible Mooney–Rivlin material with the term $\lambda (J^\gamma + J^{-\gamma})$, which grows quickly when an element is compressed or stretched. Its tangent is not positive definite for large distortions; HessianRegularization clamps the eigenvalues of $\partial^2 \Psi / \partial \boldsymbol F \partial \boldsymbol F$ at $\delta$ (energy and stress unchanged), so that Newton always moves downhill.

model_vacuum_mag = IdealMagnetic2D(μ0=μ0, χe=0.0)
model_vacuum_mech = HessianRegularization(mechano=NonlinearMooneyRivlin2D_CV(λ=1.0, μ1=1.0, μ2=0.0, α1=6.0, α2=1.0, γ=6.0), δ=1e-6)

Domains

The magnetic potential lives on the whole mesh, the displacements on the beam and on the vacuum separately. Γs is the boundary of the beam on the Interface tag, seen from the beam.

Ωdomain = Triangulation(geomodel, tags=["vacuumb", "Beam"])
Ωsolid = Triangulation(geomodel, tags=["Beam"])
Ωvacuum = Triangulation(geomodel, tags=["vacuumb"])
order = 1
dΩsolid = Measure(Ωsolid, 2 * order)
dΩvacuum = Measure(Ωvacuum, 2 * order)
Γs = BoundaryTriangulation(Ωsolid, tags="Interface")
dΓs = Measure(Γs, 4 * order)

Boundary conditions

The potential ramps with the load factor at the bottom of the box; the beam is clamped on Fixed_Solid and symmetric on Fixed_Solid_x (only $u_x = 0$). The vacuum is fixed on the outer box and symmetric on $x = 0$. FAST applies half of the potential.

φbot = FAST ? 0.2 * 5200 / 2 : 0.2 * 5200                      # A (magnetic scalar potential)
Dφ = BoundaryConditions(BoundaryCondition("phibot", φbot, Λ -> Λ), BoundaryCondition("phitop", 0.0, Λ -> Λ))
Du_solid = BoundaryConditions(BoundaryCondition("Fixed_Solid", [0.0, 0.0]),
                              BoundaryCondition("Fixed_Solid_x", [0.0, 0.0]; mask=(true, false)))

Finite element spaces and states

A staggered solve needs, for every field, the current state (⁺) and its value at the previous pass (⁻), each an FEFunction on its own trial space: the forms of a field read the other fields through them.

reffeφ = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, Float64, order)
reffeu = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, VectorValue{2,Float64}, order)

Vφ = TestFESpace(Ωdomain, reffeφ, Dφ, conformity=:H1)
Uφ⁺ = TrialFESpace(Vφ, Dφ); Uφ⁻ = TrialFESpace(Vφ, Dφ)
φh⁺ = FEFunction(Uφ⁺, zero_free_values(Uφ⁺)); φh⁻ = FEFunction(Uφ⁻, zero_free_values(Uφ⁻))

Vu_solid = TestFESpace(Ωsolid, reffeu, Du_solid, conformity=:H1)
Uu_solid⁺ = TrialFESpace(Vu_solid, Du_solid); Uu_solid⁻ = TrialFESpace(Vu_solid, Du_solid)
uh_solid⁺ = FEFunction(Uu_solid⁺, zero_free_values(Uu_solid⁺)); uh_solid⁻ = FEFunction(Uu_solid⁻, zero_free_values(Uu_solid⁻))

The vacuum follows the beam on the interface. StageBlend(uh⁻, uh⁺) is the beam displacement between its two states, $\boldsymbol u_s(\tau) = \boldsymbol u_s^- + \tau\,(\boldsymbol u_s^+ - \boldsymbol u_s^-)$, with the stage $\tau \in [0, 1]$ of the inner solve; Coupling prescribes it as Dirichlet data on the Interface nodes of the vacuum. The nodes are located once in the mesh of the beam (here they coincide, so the Dirichlet values are a copy of dof values) and updated in place at every solve.

uhsolid = StageBlend(uh_solid⁻, uh_solid⁺)
Du_vacuum = BoundaryConditions(BoundaryCondition("Fixed_Air", [0.0, 0.0]),
                               BoundaryCondition("Fixed_Air_x", [0.0, 0.0]; mask=(true, false)),
                               BoundaryCondition("Interface", Coupling(uhsolid)))
Vu_vacuum = TestFESpace(Ωvacuum, reffeu, Du_vacuum, conformity=:H1)
Uu_vacuum⁺ = TrialFESpace(Vu_vacuum, Du_vacuum); Uu_vacuum⁻ = TrialFESpace(Vu_vacuum, Du_vacuum)
uh_vacuum⁺ = FEFunction(Uu_vacuum⁺, zero_free_values(Uu_vacuum⁺)); uh_vacuum⁻ = FEFunction(Uu_vacuum⁻, zero_free_values(Uu_vacuum⁻))

uhvacuum(τ) = uh_vacuum⁻ + (uh_vacuum⁺ - uh_vacuum⁻) * τ        # the same blend written as a closure
φh(τ) = φh⁻ + (φh⁺ - φh⁻) * τ

The magnetisation direction $\boldsymbol N = (1, 0)$, as a finite element field on the beam.

Nh = interpolate_everywhere(x -> VectorValue(1.0, 0.0), TestFESpace(Ωsolid, reffeu))
@printf("Unknowns: magnetic potential %d, solid displacement %d, vacuum displacement %d (total %d)\n",
        num_free_dofs(Uφ⁺), num_free_dofs(Uu_solid⁺), num_free_dofs(Uu_vacuum⁺),
        num_free_dofs(Uφ⁺) + num_free_dofs(Uu_solid⁺) + num_free_dofs(Uu_vacuum⁺))

Maxwell traction on the interface

The magnetic stress of the vacuum acts on the beam. Its nodal forces, $f_a = \int_{\Omega_v} \boldsymbol P_v : \nabla_0 N_a \,\mathrm dV$, are assembled on a vacuum space without Dirichlet conditions; Transfer takes the values at the interface nodes into a space Usolid_int defined only on $\Gamma$ (built once, it is a gather of dof values when the nodes coincide), and the solve with the interface mass matrix $\boldsymbol M$ turns them into a traction field $\boldsymbol t_M$ with $\int_\Gamma \boldsymbol t_M \cdot \delta\boldsymbol u \,\mathrm dA = \sum_a f_a \cdot \delta\boldsymbol u_a$. (StaticLinearModel(...)(x) solves $\boldsymbol M \boldsymbol y = \boldsymbol x$ in place, reusing its factorisation.)

V_maxwell = TestFESpace(Ωvacuum, reffeu)
Pmaxw = FEFunction(V_maxwell, zero_free_values(V_maxwell))
Pmaxw_ = get_free_dof_values(Pmaxw)
Usolid_int = FESpace(Γs, reffeu)
tmaxw = interpolate_everywhere(Pmaxw, Usolid_int)
tmaxw_ = get_free_dof_values(tmaxw)
T_maxwell = Transfer(V_maxwell, Usolid_int)                    # vacuum → interface of the beam
V_traction = TestFESpace(Γs, reffeu)
mass_Γ = StaticLinearModel((u, v) -> ∫(u ⋅ v)dΓs, TrialFESpace(V_traction), V_traction, NothingBC(); backend=:compiled)

Weak forms

Each problem gets its residual and Jacobian as functions of the stage $\tau$ of its inner solve. The fields solved earlier in the same pass are read between their previous and new values (φh(τ), uhsolid(τ)), so that the inner load stepping of a problem applies the change of the other fields gradually (and can bisect it); the fields solved later are read at their last value (there $⁻$ and $⁺$ coincide).

Ψs, ∂Ψs∂F, ∂Ψs∂ℋ, ∂Ψs∂FF, ∂Ψs∂ℋF, ∂Ψs∂ℋℋ = model_solid()
Ψv, ∂Ψv∂F, ∂Ψv∂ℋ, ∂Ψv∂FF, ∂Ψv∂ℋF, ∂Ψv∂ℋℋ = model_vacuum_mag()
Ψvm, ∂Ψvm∂F, ∂Ψvm∂FF = model_vacuum_mech()
F, H, J = get_Kinematics(Kinematics(Mechano, Solid))
ℋ₀ = get_Kinematics(Kinematics(Magneto, Solid))               # ∇φ ↦ -∇φ

Problem 1, magnetic potential ($\boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0 = -\nabla_0\varphi$ gives the signs):

res_mag(τ) = (φ, vφ) ->
  -1.0 * ∫(∇(vφ) ⋅ (∂Ψs∂ℋ ∘ (F ∘ (∇(uhsolid(τ))'), ℋ₀ ∘ (∇(φ)), Nh)))dΩsolid -
  ∫(∇(vφ) ⋅ (∂Ψv∂ℋ ∘ (F ∘ (∇(uhvacuum(τ))'), ℋ₀ ∘ (∇(φ)))))dΩvacuum
jac_mag(τ) = (φ, dφ, vφ) ->
  ∫(∇(vφ) ⋅ ((∂Ψs∂ℋℋ ∘ (F ∘ (∇(uhsolid(τ))'), ℋ₀ ∘ (∇(φ)), Nh)) ⋅ ∇(dφ)))dΩsolid +
  ∫(∇(vφ) ⋅ ((∂Ψv∂ℋℋ ∘ (F ∘ (∇(uhvacuum(τ))'), ℋ₀ ∘ (∇(φ)))) ⋅ ∇(dφ)))dΩvacuum

Problem 2, mechanics of the beam. Before returning the form, res_mech(τ) updates the Maxwell traction with the current fields (the three steps of the previous section); it is a load, so it is not differentiated in the Jacobian.

res_mech(τ) = begin
  assemble_vector!(v -> ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ (∂Ψv∂F ∘ (F ∘ (∇(uhvacuum(τ))'), ℋ₀ ∘ (∇(φh(τ))))))dΩvacuum, Pmaxw_, V_maxwell)
  transfer!(tmaxw_, T_maxwell, Pmaxw)
  mass_Γ(tmaxw_)
  (u, v) -> ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ (∂Ψs∂F ∘ (F ∘ (∇(u)'), ℋ₀ ∘ (∇(φh(τ))), Nh)))dΩsolid + ∫(v ⋅ tmaxw)dΓs
end
jac_mech(τ) = (u, du, v) -> ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ ((∂Ψs∂FF ∘ (F ∘ (∇(u)'), ℋ₀ ∘ (∇(φh(τ))), Nh)) ⋅² (∇(du)')))dΩsolid

Problem 3, motion of the vacuum:

res_vac(τ) = (u, v) -> ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ (∂Ψvm∂F ∘ (F ∘ (∇(u)'))))dΩvacuum
jac_vac(τ) = (u, du, v) -> ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ ((∂Ψvm∂FF ∘ (F ∘ (∇(u)'))) ⋅² (∇(du)')))dΩvacuum

Solvers and computational models

One StaticNonlinearModel per problem, each solving for its own state (xh=), with the tolerances of the paper. Two of them need no Newton iteration at all, because the Dirichlet projection of ProjectDirichlet=true (see Solve) is a linear solve with the tangent that already does the work:

  • the magnetic problem is linear in $\varphi$ once the displacements are fixed, so the projection of the new potential on the box gives its solution and Newton converges at iteration 0;
  • the motion of the vacuum is not solved to equilibrium: rtol=2 accepts the starting point, the projection that moves the interface to the new position of the beam (one linear solve per pass), which is all the vacuum needs, a valid mesh that follows the beam (maxiter=1 only bounds what is never used).

The beam is solved to a 1 % relative residual, as in the paper (the coupling error of a staggered step is larger than that). The regression values in Checks rest on these loosely converged iterates; they reproduce across machines because the number of iterations is fixed by the data: HyperFEM's test suite asserts the FAST values below at rtol = 1e-6 on Linux. All of them, and the interface mass matrix above, assemble with the compiled backend (backend=:compiled, tutorial 01): at every pass the forms are rebound to the new stage and to the current states of the other fields, never compiled again.

nls_mag = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=20, atol=1e-9, rtol=1e-8, verbose=false)
cm_mag = StaticNonlinearModel(res_mag, jac_mag, Uφ⁺, Vφ, Dφ; nls=nls_mag, xh=φh⁺, backend=:compiled)
nls_mech = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=20, atol=1e-6, rtol=1e-2, verbose=false)
cm_mech = StaticNonlinearModel(res_mech, jac_mech, Uu_solid⁺, Vu_solid, Du_solid; nls=nls_mech, xh=uh_solid⁺, backend=:compiled)
nls_vac = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=1, rtol=2, verbose=false)
cm_vac = StaticNonlinearModel(res_vac, jac_vac, Uu_vacuum⁺, Vu_vacuum, Du_vacuum; nls=nls_vac, xh=uh_vacuum⁺, backend=:compiled)

StaggeredModel takes the three models (in the order they are solved), their states and the lagged states.

comp_model = StaggeredModel((cm_mag, cm_mech, cm_vac), (φh⁺, uh_solid⁺, uh_vacuum⁺), (φh⁻, uh_solid⁻, uh_vacuum⁻))

Post-processing

on_step runs after every accepted step: it writes the deformed beam and the vacuum with the field $\boldsymbol{\mathcal H}_0 = -\nabla_0\varphi$.

pvd_solid = createpvd(joinpath(outdir, "solid"))
pvd_vacuum = createpvd(joinpath(outdir, "vacuum"))
nvtk = Ref(0)
function write_step(τ, Δτ, nsub)
  n = (nvtk[] += 1)
  pvd_solid[τ] = createvtk(Ωsolid, joinpath(outdir, "solid_$n"), cellfields=["u" => uh_solid⁺, "H0" => -∇(φh⁺)])
  pvd_vacuum[τ] = createvtk(Ωvacuum, joinpath(outdir, "vacuum_$n"), cellfields=["u" => uh_vacuum⁺, "H0" => -∇(φh⁺)])
end

Solve

StaggeredStepping controls the outer loop: nsteps equal load steps, and in each one max_substeps passes through the three problems. Without coupling_tol this is the fixed loop: every step makes exactly that number of passes, whatever the remaining coupling error (the paper's setting; see Going further for the converged loop). kargsolve holds the keywords of the inner solve! of each problem: one load increment per pass, bisected up to five times if Newton fails, and ProjectDirichlet=true for the two problems with moving Dirichlet data (the potential and the interface of the vacuum).

stepping = FAST ? StaggeredStepping(nsteps=4, max_substeps=1, maxbisec=1) :
                  StaggeredStepping(nsteps=20, max_substeps=2, maxbisec=1)
kargsolve = ((stepping=LoadStepping(nsteps=1, maxbisec=5), ProjectDirichlet=true),
             (stepping=LoadStepping(nsteps=1, maxbisec=5),),
             (stepping=LoadStepping(nsteps=1, maxbisec=5), ProjectDirichlet=true))
x, flag = solve!(comp_model; stepping=stepping, kargsolve=kargsolve, verbose=true, on_step=write_step)
savepvd(pvd_solid); savepvd(pvd_vacuum)

get_log returns the record of the solve: per accepted step, the passes, the coupling error of every field after the last pass (the relative change of its free values in that pass) and the time spent in each problem. With one pass per step that change is just the increment of the step; from the second pass on it measures how far the three fields still are from agreeing with each other.

slog = get_log(comp_model)
println(slog)
tf = reduce(+, slog.times)
@printf("time in the inner solves: magnetic %.1f s, beam %.1f s, vacuum %.1f s\n", tf...)
@printf("coupling error after the last pass of the last step: magnetic %.1e, beam %.1e, vacuum %.1e\n", slog.errors[end]...)

Checks

Displacement of the tip of the beam ($x = 50$ mm), averaged over the tip face.

χtip = CellField(x -> x[1] > 0.05 - 1e-6 ? 1.0 : 0.0, Γs)
u_tip = sum(∫(χtip * uh_solid⁺)dΓs) / sum(∫(χtip)dΓs)
@printf("tip displacement: (%.4f, %.4f) mm\n", 1e3 * u_tip[1], 1e3 * u_tip[2])

(b) The vacuum follows the beam: on the interface its displacement equals the one of the beam. Both are taken to the interface space with Transfer (the beam's nodes there are free or clamped, the vacuum's are all Dirichlet).

us_Γ = zeros(num_free_dofs(Usolid_int)); uv_Γ = zeros(num_free_dofs(Usolid_int))
transfer!(us_Γ, Transfer(Vu_solid, Usolid_int), uh_solid⁺)
transfer!(uv_Γ, Transfer(Vu_vacuum, Usolid_int), uh_vacuum⁺)
gap = maximum(abs.(uv_Γ .- us_Γ)) / maximum(abs.(us_Γ))
println("interface gap |u_vacuum - u_solid| / max|u_solid| = $gap")

(b) Neither mesh is inverted: the smallest $J$ at the quadrature points.

Jmin(uh, dΩ) = minimum(minimum, (J ∘ (F ∘ ∇(uh)'))(get_cell_points(dΩ)))
Jmin_s, Jmin_v = Jmin(uh_solid⁺, dΩsolid), Jmin(uh_vacuum⁺, dΩvacuum)
@printf("min J: beam %.4f, vacuum %.4f\n", Jmin_s, Jmin_v)

nφ, ns, nv = norm(get_free_dof_values(φh⁺)), norm(get_free_dof_values(uh_solid⁺)), norm(get_free_dof_values(uh_vacuum⁺))
@printf("‖φ‖ = %.10e   ‖u_s‖ = %.10e   ‖u_v‖ = %.10e   u_tip = (%.10e, %.10e)\n", nφ, ns, nv, u_tip[1], u_tip[2])

@assert flag == 1 "an inner solve did not converge"
@assert length(slog.τ) == stepping.nsteps && all(==(stepping.max_substeps), slog.nsub) "unexpected number of steps or passes"
@assert gap < 1e-10 "the vacuum does not follow the beam on the interface (gap $gap)"
@assert Jmin_s > 0 && Jmin_v > 0 "inverted elements"
@assert u_tip[2] < 0 && u_tip[1] < 0 "the tip should move down and towards the clamp (clockwise rotation)"

(c) Regression. FAST is the reduced case of the test suite of HyperFEM (test/SimulationsTests/HMREVacuumSimulation.jl, half of the potential, 4 steps of one pass); the same values here show that StaggeredStepping reproduces it. The full case: our own values (HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8, Julia 1.13.0, macOS arm64).

if FAST
  @assert isapprox(nφ, 2.0940145799e4; rtol=1e-6) "‖φ‖ = $nφ differs from the HyperFEM test"
  @assert isapprox(ns, 1.0426282896e-1; rtol=1e-6) "‖u_s‖ = $ns differs from the HyperFEM test"
  @assert isapprox(nv, 2.4014448097e-1; rtol=1e-6) "‖u_v‖ = $nv differs from the HyperFEM test"
else
  REF_PHI, REF_US, REF_UV, REF_TIP = 4.2100933777e4, 1.9454136102e-1, 4.5495058874e-1, -1.5853670013e-2
  @assert isapprox(nφ, REF_PHI; rtol=1e-5) "‖φ‖ = $nφ differs from the reference"
  @assert isapprox(ns, REF_US; rtol=1e-5) "‖u_s‖ = $ns differs from the reference"

Going further

  • Converged staggered loop. With a coupling tolerance every step makes passes until the relative change of every field is below it, and a step that does not converge in max_substeps passes is rejected and bisected. The plain iteration converges slowly here (the field and the beam pull on each other); a quasi-Newton update of the beam displacement built from the passes of the step, IQNILS(), accelerates it (one relaxation per field):
    stepping = StaggeredStepping(nsteps=20, max_substeps=30, coupling_tol=1e-6, maxbisec=4,
                             relaxation=(:none, IQNILS(), :none))
    get_log(comp_model) then shows how many passes every step needed. Compare the tip deflection with the fixed loop.
  • Aitken() is a cheaper scalar relaxation; StaggeredStepping(grow=1.5) lets the steps grow when they converge in few passes.
