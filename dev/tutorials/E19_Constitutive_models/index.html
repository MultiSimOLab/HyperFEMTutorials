Tutorial 19: Constitutive models: energies, stresses and tangents

Source · Notebook · nbviewer

Every simulation of HyperFEM starts from a constitutive model: a small object that returns the stored energy $\Psi(\boldsymbol F)$, the first Piola–Kirchhoff stress $\partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi$ and the tangent $\partial^2_{\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol F}\Psi$ as plain Julia functions. This tutorial uses them without finite elements: it evaluates several hyperelastic models in the three classical homogeneous tests (uniaxial tension, equibiaxial tension and simple shear), checks them against closed-form solutions and against their own derivatives, and writes a new model with the same interface.

What you will learn

  • the interface of the PhysicalModels: Ψ, ∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF = model(), and the extra arguments of the anisotropic models (the fibre directions);
  • the catalogue of 3D hyperelastic models: NeoHookean3D, MooneyRivlin3D, Yeoh3D, EightChain, IncompressibleNeoHookean3D, TransverseIsotropy3D, HGO_1Fiber, HGO_4Fibers, and how to add models with +;
  • the kinematics object (Kinematics, get_Kinematics, getIsoInvariants);
  • homogeneous tests: the incompressible closed forms and the compressible ones (lateral faces free of traction, found with a scalar Newton iteration that uses the tangent);
  • how to verify a model (derivatives, objectivity, stress-free reference state) and how to write your own;
  • why the models are fast inside the compiled kernels: they are allocation-free isbits closures.

Measured on a Mac M5 Max (Julia 1.13.0, 4 Julia threads, 4 BLAS threads; HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8): about 7 s including package loading and compilation; there is no FAST variant (the reduced and the full runs are the same, the computation takes milliseconds). No degrees of freedom: only point evaluations.

Problem statement

A hyperelastic material is defined by a stored energy per unit reference volume $\Psi(\boldsymbol F)$ of the deformation gradient $\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I + \nabla_0\boldsymbol u$. The first Piola–Kirchhoff (nominal) stress and the tangent used by Newton's method are

\[\boldsymbol P = \frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial \boldsymbol F}, \qquad \mathbb A = \frac{\partial^2 \Psi}{\partial \boldsymbol F\,\partial \boldsymbol F}, \qquad \mathrm d\boldsymbol P = \mathbb A : \mathrm d\boldsymbol F .\]

Most models are written with the cofactor $\boldsymbol H = J\boldsymbol F^{-T}$ and the Jacobian $J = \det\boldsymbol F$, and with the invariants $I_1 = \boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F$ and $I_2 = \boldsymbol H:\boldsymbol H$.

Homogeneous deformations, with $\boldsymbol e_i$ the Cartesian basis:

  • uniaxial tension, $\boldsymbol F = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda, \lambda_t, \lambda_t)$ with $P_{22} = P_{33} = 0$;
  • equibiaxial tension, $\boldsymbol F = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda, \lambda, \lambda_3)$ with $P_{33} = 0$;
  • simple shear, $\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I + \gamma\, \boldsymbol e_1 \otimes \boldsymbol e_2$ ($J = 1$).

For an incompressible material ($J = 1$, $\lambda_t = \lambda^{-1/2}$, $\lambda_3 = \lambda^{-2}$) with $\Psi = W(I_1, I_2)$, $W_1 = \partial W/\partial I_1$ and $W_2 = \partial W/\partial I_2$, the nominal stresses are (Ogden, Non-linear elastic deformations, 1984, §7.2)

\[P_{\mathrm{uni}} = 2\left(\lambda - \lambda^{-2}\right)\left(W_1 + \lambda^{-1} W_2\right), \qquad P_{\mathrm{equi}} = 2\left(\lambda - \lambda^{-5}\right)\left(W_1 + \lambda^{2} W_2\right), \qquad P_{12} = 2\gamma\,(W_1 + W_2).\]

The models of HyperFEM are compressible: they add volumetric terms of $J$ to $W$. At $J = 1$ those terms only contribute stresses proportional to $\boldsymbol F^{-T}$, which an incompressible material absorbs in its pressure: $\boldsymbol P = \hat{\boldsymbol P}(\boldsymbol F) - p\,\boldsymbol F^{-T}$, with $p$ given by the traction-free lateral faces. That is how the closed forms are checked below.

Kinematics

Kinematics(Mechano, Solid) holds the maps used by the weak forms: $\boldsymbol F(\nabla\boldsymbol u)$, $\boldsymbol H(\boldsymbol F)$ and $J(\boldsymbol F)$. In a weak form they are composed with the FE gradient, F ∘ ∇(u)' (Gridap's ∇(u) is the transpose of $\nabla_0\boldsymbol u$); here we call them with a tensor. getIsoInvariants gives $I_1$, $I_2$ and $I_3 = J$.

kinematics = Kinematics(Mechano, Solid)
Fmap, Hmap, Jmap = get_Kinematics(kinematics)
I1map, I2map, I3map = getIsoInvariants(kinematics)
∇u = TensorValue(0.10, 0.02, 0.00, 0.05, -0.03, 0.01, 0.00, 0.02, 0.04)
F = Fmap(∇u)
println("F = ", F)
println("J = ", Jmap(F), ",  I1 = ", I1map(F), ",  I2 = ", I2map(F))
@assert Fmap(∇u) == one(∇u) + ∇u
@assert norm(Hmap(F) - det(F) * inv(F)') < 1e-14 * norm(Hmap(F)) "H is the cofactor J F⁻ᵀ"
@assert abs(I2map(F) - Hmap(F) ⊙ Hmap(F)) < 1e-14 * I2map(F)

A first model

A model is a struct with its parameters; calling it with no arguments returns three functions of $\boldsymbol F$ (closures that capture the parameters). The stress is a TensorValue{3,3} and the tangent a FourthOrderTensorValue{3}, applied to an increment with the double contraction ⋅², as in the jacobian of tutorial 02.

nh = NeoHookean3D(λ=100.0, μ=1.0)                              # kPa
Ψ, ∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF = nh()
println("Ψ(F) = ", Ψ(F), " kPa")
println("P(F) = ", ∂Ψ∂F(F))
println(typeof(∂Ψ∂FF(F)))
dF = 1e-6 * TensorValue(1.0, -2.0, 0.5, 0.3, 1.0, -0.7, 0.2, 0.4, -1.0)
println("‖P(F + dF) - P(F) - A:dF‖ / ‖A:dF‖ = ", norm(∂Ψ∂F(F + dF) - ∂Ψ∂F(F) - ∂Ψ∂FF(F) ⋅² dF) / norm(∂Ψ∂FF(F) ⋅² dF))

The model catalogue

The 3D hyperelastic models of HyperFEM 0.3.0 (their code is in HyperFEM's src/PhysicalModels/MechanicalModels.jl; some, like ?Yeoh3D or ?EightChain, have docstrings), all with a shear modulus close to 1 kPa and a bulk penalty $\lambda = 100$ kPa (nearly incompressible):

  • NeoHookean3D(λ, μ): $\tfrac{\mu}{2}(I_1-3) - \mu\ln J + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}(J-1)^2$;
  • MooneyRivlin3D(λ, μ1, μ2): $\tfrac{\mu_1}{2}(I_1-3) + \tfrac{\mu_2}{2}(I_2-3) - (\mu_1+2\mu_2)\ln J + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}(J-1)^2$;
  • Yeoh3D(λ, C10, C20, C30): $\sum_{k} C_{k0}(I_1-3)^k - 2C_{10}\ln J + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}(J-1)^2$;
  • EightChain(μ, N): Arruda–Boyce in the isochoric invariant $\bar I_1 = J^{-2/3} I_1$ only, so it needs a volumetric term: here + VolumetricEnergy(λ), $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(J-1)^2$;
  • IncompressibleNeoHookean3D(λ, μ, δ): neo-Hookean in $\bar I_1$ with a regularised $J$ (parameter $\delta$) and a penalty $\lambda$. HyperFEM has no exactly incompressible (mixed $u$–$p$) model: incompressibility is always a penalty;
  • fibre models, functions of $(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol N)$ with $\boldsymbol N$ the fibre direction in the reference configuration: TransverseIsotropy3D(μ, α1, α2), polyconvex in $I_4 = |\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol N|^2$ and $I_5 = |\boldsymbol H\boldsymbol N|^2$, and the Holzapfel–Gasser–Ogden fibre energy HGO_1Fiber(c1, c2), $\tfrac{c_1}{4c_2}\left(e^{c_2(I_4-1)^2}-1\right)$ (HGO_4Fibers has four families). They have no volumetric term of their own and are added to an isotropic matrix.

Adding models. + composes an isotropic model with another isotropic one (the result is isotropic) or with an anisotropic one (the result takes the fibre directions as extra arguments). Gent exists only in 2D (Gent2D, plane strain); a 3D Gent model is written below as the example of a user model.

const N_fibre = e1                                             # fibres along the loading direction
const N4 = (e1, VectorValue(0.0, 1.0, 1.0), VectorValue(1.0, 1.0, 0.0), VectorValue(1.0, 0.0, 1.0))

Writing your own model

A model is any subtype of Mechano callable with no arguments that returns (Ψ, ∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF). That is all that residual, jacobian and energy use, so the new model works in every mechanical tutorial (02, 03, 13–15). Subtyping the (non-exported) HyperFEM.PhysicalModels.IsoElastic instead also enables + with the other models.

The compressible Gent model in 3D, with $J_m$ the limit of $I_1 - 3$ (finite extensibility):

\[\Psi = -\frac{\mu J_m}{2}\ln\left(1 - \frac{I_1-3}{J_m}\right) - \mu\ln J + \frac{\lambda}{2}(J-1)^2 , \qquad \boldsymbol P = \frac{\mu}{g}\boldsymbol F + \Psi_J \boldsymbol H , \qquad g = 1 - \frac{I_1-3}{J_m}, \quad \Psi_J = -\frac{\mu}{J} + \lambda (J-1),\]

\[\mathbb A = \frac{\mu}{g}\,\mathbb I + \frac{2\mu}{J_m g^2}\,\boldsymbol F\otimes\boldsymbol F + \left(\frac{\mu}{J^2} + \lambda\right)\boldsymbol H\otimes\boldsymbol H + \Psi_J\,\frac{\partial\boldsymbol H}{\partial\boldsymbol F} .\]

𝕀3D is the fourth-order identity and ×ᵢ⁴(F) is $\partial\boldsymbol H/\partial\boldsymbol F$ (both exported by HyperFEM). The three functions read only their argument and the parameters captured from the struct, never a non-constant global variable (a non-constant global has an unknown type, so every call that reads it is resolved at run time and is many times slower); so they compile to allocation-free code.

struct Gent3D <: HyperFEM.PhysicalModels.IsoElastic
  μ::Float64
  Jm::Float64
  λ::Float64
end
Gent3D(; μ::Float64, Jm::Float64, λ::Float64) = Gent3D(μ, Jm, λ)

function (obj::Gent3D)()
  μ, Jm, λ = obj.μ, obj.Jm, obj.λ
  J(F) = det(F)
  H(F) = det(F) * inv(F)'
  g(F) = 1.0 - (tr(F' * F) - 3.0) / Jm
  ΨJ(F) = -μ / J(F) + λ * (J(F) - 1.0)
  Ψ(F) = -0.5 * μ * Jm * log(g(F)) - μ * log(J(F)) + 0.5 * λ * (J(F) - 1.0)^2
  ∂Ψ∂F(F) = (μ / g(F)) * F + ΨJ(F) * H(F)
  ∂Ψ∂FF(F) = (μ / g(F)) * 𝕀3D + (2.0 * μ / (Jm * g(F)^2)) * (F ⊗ F) +
             (μ / J(F)^2 + λ) * (H(F) ⊗ H(F)) + ΨJ(F) * ×ᵢ⁴(F)
  return (Ψ, ∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF)
end

A second user model: a fibre-reinforced material that adds an isotropic matrix and a fibre model, like +, but with the types of both parts as parameters of the struct and with the fibre arguments declared as Vararg{Any,K}. That declaration makes Julia compile one method per number of fibres; the reason is explained in the Efficiency section.

struct FibreReinforced{M<:Mechano,A<:Mechano} <: Mechano
  matrix::M
  fibres::A
end

function (obj::FibreReinforced)()
  Ψm, Pm, Am = obj.matrix()
  Ψf, Pf, Af = obj.fibres()
  Ψ(F, N::Vararg{Any,K}) where {K} = Ψm(F) + Ψf(F, N...)
  ∂Ψ∂F(F, N::Vararg{Any,K}) where {K} = Pm(F) + Pf(F, N...)
  ∂Ψ∂FF(F, N::Vararg{Any,K}) where {K} = Am(F) + Af(F, N...)
  return (Ψ, ∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF)
end

Homogeneous tests

For a compressible model the lateral stretch is unknown. The scalar equation $P_{22}(\lambda_t) = 0$ is solved by Newton's method with the model's own tangent, $\mathrm dP_{22}/\mathrm d\lambda_t = [\mathbb A : (\boldsymbol e_2\otimes\boldsymbol e_2 + \boldsymbol e_3\otimes\boldsymbol e_3)]_{22}$; for the equibiaxial test the unknown is $\lambda_3$ and the equation $P_{33} = 0$. The fibres of the anisotropic models are along $\boldsymbol e_1$, so $\lambda_2 = \lambda_3$ still holds in uniaxial tension (with four fibre families it does not: those models are only used in the checks).

function lateral_newton(∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF, Fof, dFda, i, a, args; tol=1e-13, maxiter=30)
  for _ in 1:maxiter
    F = Fof(a)
    r = ∂Ψ∂F(F, args...)[i, i]
    abs(r) < tol && return a, F
    a -= r / (∂Ψ∂FF(F, args...) ⋅² dFda)[i, i]
  end
  error("lateral equilibrium did not converge")
end

uniaxial(∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF, λ, args) = lateral_newton(∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF, a -> diagF(λ, a, a), e2 ⊗ e2 + e3 ⊗ e3, 2, 1 / sqrt(λ), args)
equibiaxial(∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF, λ, args) = lateral_newton(∂Ψ∂F, ∂Ψ∂FF, a -> diagF(λ, λ, a), e3 ⊗ e3, 3, 1 / λ^2, args)
shearF(γ) = one(TensorValue{3,3,Float64}) + γ * (e1 ⊗ e2)

stretches = collect(1.0:0.1:2.0)
shears = collect(0.0:0.1:1.0)
iso_models = filter(m -> length(m.args) <= 1, models)    # the four-fibre models are only checked
table_uni = zeros(length(stretches), length(iso_models))
table_lat = zeros(length(stretches), length(iso_models))
table_equi = zeros(length(stretches), length(iso_models))
table_shear = zeros(length(shears), length(iso_models))
for (j, m) in enumerate(iso_models)
  _, Pm, Am = m.model()
  for (k, λ) in enumerate(stretches)
    λt, Fu = uniaxial(Pm, Am, λ, m.args)
    table_uni[k, j] = Pm(Fu, m.args...)[1, 1]
    table_lat[k, j] = λt
    _, Fe = equibiaxial(Pm, Am, λ, m.args)
    table_equi[k, j] = Pm(Fe, m.args...)[1, 1]
  end
  for (k, γ) in enumerate(shears)
    table_shear[k, j] = Pm(shearF(γ), m.args...)[1, 2]
  end
end

function print_table(title, xname, x, T, names)
  println("\n", title)
  @printf("%6s", xname)
  foreach(n -> @printf(" %19s", n), names)
  println()
  for k in eachindex(x)
    @printf("%6.2f", x[k])
    foreach(v -> @printf(" %19.6f", v), T[k, :])
    println()
  end
end

    println(io, join([xname; names], ","))
    for k in eachindex(x)

end
colnames = [m.name for m in iso_models]
print_table("Uniaxial tension, lateral faces free: nominal stress P11 (kPa)", "λ", stretches, table_uni, colnames)
print_table("Equibiaxial tension, P33 = 0: nominal stress P11 (kPa)", "λ", stretches, table_equi, colnames)
print_table("Simple shear: nominal shear stress P12 (kPa)", "γ", shears, table_shear, colnames)

Reading the tables: all the isotropic models share the initial slope ($3\mu$ in uniaxial tension, $6\mu$ in equibiaxial tension and $\mu$ in shear, with $\mu = 1$ kPa) and differ at large stretch: Mooney–Rivlin softens in uniaxial tension and stiffens in equibiaxial tension, Yeoh, Gent and the eight-chain model stiffen. The fibre models are much stiffer along the fibre; in simple shear the fibre $\boldsymbol N = \boldsymbol e_1$ is not stretched ($\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol e_1 = \boldsymbol e_1$), so HGO_1Fiber adds nothing, whereas TransverseIsotropy3D does, through $I_5$.

Checks

(a) Closed forms. Incompressible tests: the model is evaluated at the isochoric $\boldsymbol F$ and the pressure $p$ is eliminated with the traction-free lateral faces. The derivatives $W_1$, $W_2$ of the isochoric part of each model are written by hand (neo-Hookean $W_1 = \mu/2$; Mooney–Rivlin $W_1 = \mu_1/2$, $W_2 = \mu_2/2$; Yeoh $W_1 = C_{10} + 2C_{20}(I_1-3) + 3C_{30}(I_1-3)^2$; Gent $W_1 = \mu/(2g)$).

function incompressible_uniaxial(P, λ)
  s = 1 / sqrt(λ)
  P̂ = P(diagF(λ, s, s))
  p = P̂[2, 2] * s                                              # P22 = P̂22 - p/s = 0
  P̂[1, 1] - p / λ
end
function incompressible_equibiaxial(P, λ)
  s = 1 / λ^2
  P̂ = P(diagF(λ, λ, s))
  p = P̂[3, 3] * s
  P̂[1, 1] - p / λ
end
incompressible_shear(P, γ) = P(shearF(γ))[1, 2]                # F⁻ᵀ has no 12 component: p does not enter

closed_forms = [
  ("NeoHookean3D",   NeoHookean3D(λ=100.0, μ=1.0),                    (I1, I2) -> (0.5, 0.0)),
  ("MooneyRivlin3D", MooneyRivlin3D(λ=100.0, μ1=0.7, μ2=0.3),         (I1, I2) -> (0.35, 0.15)),
  ("Yeoh3D",         Yeoh3D(λ=100.0, C10=0.5, C20=0.02, C30=0.001),    (I1, I2) -> (0.5 + 0.04 * (I1 - 3) + 0.003 * (I1 - 3)^2, 0.0)),
  ("Gent3D (user)",  Gent3D(μ=1.0, Jm=20.0, λ=100.0),                  (I1, I2) -> (0.5 / (1 - (I1 - 3) / 20.0), 0.0)),
]
max_err_a = 0.0
for (name, model, W) in closed_forms
  _, Pm, _ = model()
  err = 0.0
  for λ in stretches[2:end]
    W1, W2 = W(λ^2 + 2 / λ, 2λ + 1 / λ^2)
    Pex = 2 * (λ - λ^-2) * (W1 + W2 / λ)
    err = max(err, abs(incompressible_uniaxial(Pm, λ) - Pex) / abs(Pex))
    W1, W2 = W(2λ^2 + λ^-4, λ^4 + 2 / λ^2)
    Pex = 2 * (λ - λ^-5) * (W1 + λ^2 * W2)
    err = max(err, abs(incompressible_equibiaxial(Pm, λ) - Pex) / abs(Pex))
  end
  for γ in shears[2:end]
    W1, W2 = W(3 + γ^2, 3 + γ^2)
    Pex = 2γ * (W1 + W2)
    err = max(err, abs(incompressible_shear(Pm, γ) - Pex) / abs(Pex))
  end
  @printf("closed forms, %-15s max relative error %.2e\n", name, err)
  global max_err_a = max(max_err_a, err)
end
@assert max_err_a < 1e-10 "incompressible closed forms not reproduced (max error $max_err_a)"

The compressible neo-Hookean in uniaxial tension also has a closed form (tutorial 02): $J$ is the positive root of $\lambda J^2 - (\lambda - \mu/\lambda_1)J - \mu = 0$ and $P_{11} = \mu(\lambda_1 - J/\lambda_1^2)$. The nearly incompressible models are close to the incompressible curve: the difference is of order $\mu/\lambda$.

J_nh(λ₁; λ=100.0, μ=1.0) = ((λ - μ / λ₁) + sqrt((λ - μ / λ₁)^2 + 4λ * μ)) / (2λ)
P11_nh(λ₁; μ=1.0) = μ * (λ₁ - J_nh(λ₁) / λ₁^2)
err_nh = maximum(abs.(table_uni[2:end, 1] .- P11_nh.(stretches[2:end])) ./ P11_nh.(stretches[2:end]))
println("compressible neo-Hookean, uniaxial: max relative error $(@sprintf("%.2e", err_nh))")
@assert err_nh < 1e-10 "compressible neo-Hookean uniaxial curve away from the closed form"
gap = maximum(abs.(table_uni[2:end, 1] .- 1.0 .* (stretches[2:end] .- stretches[2:end] .^ -2)) ./ table_uni[2:end, 1])
println("compressible (λ = 100 μ) vs incompressible neo-Hookean, uniaxial: max relative difference $(@sprintf("%.2e", gap))")
@assert gap < 0.05

(b) Invariants of every model. At three states (small strain, large strain, compression), the stress must be the derivative of the energy and the tangent the derivative of the stress. Both are checked against central finite differences with Richardson extrapolation along the nine directions $\boldsymbol e_i\otimes\boldsymbol e_j$ (relative error of the whole Jacobian, tolerance 1e-6). Also: the tangent has the major symmetry of a second derivative; the energy is objective, $\Psi(\boldsymbol Q\boldsymbol F) = \Psi(\boldsymbol F)$ and $\boldsymbol P(\boldsymbol Q\boldsymbol F) = \boldsymbol Q\boldsymbol P(\boldsymbol F)$ for a rotation $\boldsymbol Q$ (the fibres are referential); and the reference state is free of energy and stress.

const Fs = (small=one(TensorValue{3,3,Float64}) + 1e-3 * TensorValue(1.0:9.0...),
            large=TensorValue(1.3, 0.1, 0.0, 0.2, 0.9, -0.1, 0.0, 0.15, 1.1),
            compression=TensorValue(0.5, 0.02, 0.0, 0.01, 0.6, 0.0, 0.0, 0.03, 0.67))
const Ebasis = ntuple(m -> TensorValue(ntuple(n -> n == m ? 1.0 : 0.0, 9)), 9)
const Q = let a = VectorValue(1.0, 2.0, 3.0) / sqrt(14.0), θ = 0.7     # rotation (Rodrigues)
  K = TensorValue(0.0, a[3], -a[2], -a[3], 0.0, a[1], a[2], -a[1], 0.0)
  one(K) + sin(θ) * K + (1 - cos(θ)) * (K ⋅ K)
end

function fd(f, F, dF; h=1e-4)                                  # Richardson-extrapolated central difference
  d(δ) = (f(F + δ * dF) - f(F - δ * dF)) / (2δ)
  (4 * d(h / 2) - d(h)) / 3
end
function derivative_errors(Ψ, P, A, F, args)
  eP = eA = nP = nA = 0.0
  for E in Ebasis
    dΨ = fd(G -> Ψ(G, args...), F, E)
    eP += (P(F, args...) ⊙ E - dΨ)^2
    nP += dΨ^2
    dP = fd(G -> P(G, args...), F, E)
    eA += norm(A(F, args...) ⋅² E - dP)^2
    nA += norm(dP)^2
  end
  sqrt(eP / nP), sqrt(eA / nA)
end
function symmetry_error(A, F, args)
  B, C = TensorValue(1.0:9.0...), TensorValue(0.3, -1.0, 2.0, 0.5, 0.1, -0.4, 1.5, 0.2, -0.6)
  AF = A(F, args...)
  abs(C ⊙ (AF ⋅² B) - B ⊙ (AF ⋅² C)) / abs(C ⊙ (AF ⋅² B))
end

println("\nmodel                 P vs dΨ/dF   A vs dP/dF   A symmetry   objectivity  Ψ(I), |P(I)|")
for m in models
  Ψm, Pm, Am = m.model()
  eP = eA = es = eo = 0.0
  for (state, Fm) in pairs(Fs)
    a, b = derivative_errors(Ψm, Pm, Am, Fm, m.args)
    eP, eA = max(eP, a), max(eA, b)
    es = max(es, symmetry_error(Am, Fm, m.args))
    state == :small && continue                                # Ψ ≈ 1e-6: relative round-off too large
    eo = max(eo, abs(Ψm(Q ⋅ Fm, m.args...) - Ψm(Fm, m.args...)) / abs(Ψm(Fm, m.args...)),
                 norm(Pm(Q ⋅ Fm, m.args...) - Q ⋅ Pm(Fm, m.args...)) / norm(Pm(Fm, m.args...)))
  end
  Id = one(TensorValue{3,3,Float64})
  rest = max(abs(Ψm(Id, m.args...)), norm(Pm(Id, m.args...)))
  @printf("%-20s  %.2e     %.2e     %.2e     %.2e     %.2e\n", m.name, eP, eA, es, eo, rest)
  @assert eP < 1e-6 "$(m.name): stress is not the derivative of the energy ($eP)"
  @assert eA < 1e-6 "$(m.name): tangent is not the derivative of the stress ($eA)"
  @assert es < 1e-12 "$(m.name): tangent without major symmetry ($es)"
  @assert eo < 1e-12 "$(m.name): not objective ($eo)"
  @assert rest < 1e-12 "$(m.name): energy or stress at F = I ($rest)"
end

Efficiency

The FE forms call these functions at every quadrature point (∂Ψ∂F ∘ (F ∘ ∇(u)', args...)), and the compiled backend of HyperFEMKernels captures the forms, closures included, in type-specialised loops. That is fast because a model is an immutable struct of numbers and its functions are closures over those numbers: isbits values that the compiler inlines, with no heap allocation per evaluation. The check below measures the allocations of one stress and one tangent evaluation after compilation (in a function, so that the measurement does not include the dynamic call from global scope) and the time per evaluation. Both are printed, not asserted: whether a closure is inlined depends on the compiler's heuristics, which change between Julia versions and platforms (the table below was measured with Julia 1.13 on macOS arm64), so a zero-allocation assertion could fail on another machine for reasons that have nothing to do with the model. Composite models (+) and HGO_4Fibers, whose parameters are Vectors, are not isbits structs, but their stress functions still do not allocate, with one exception (measured).

The exception: + with four fibre families. In HyperFEM 0.3.0, nh + HGO_4Fibers(...) allocates at every evaluation: 720 bytes per stress and 1920 bytes per tangent, and its tangent takes about twice as long as that of the same material written as FibreReinforced (663 ns against 327 ns in our measurement). This is a known issue of the library, and a fix is pending in HyperFEM; the model is correct (it passes every check above) and remains a valid choice. + builds (F, N...) -> Ψ1(F) + Ψ2(F, N...), and Julia does not specialise a function on a Vararg argument that it only passes on to another function (Julia manual, Performance tips, «Be aware of when Julia avoids specializing»): the call with four fibres goes through a generic method. With one fibre the compiler still inlines it. The lesson for your own models is in FibreReinforced above: declaring N::Vararg{Any,K} forces the specialisation, with the same energy and stresses and no allocation. Writing the sum of the two models with explicit arguments has the same effect.

function allocations(f, F, args)
  f(F, args...)
  @allocated f(F, args...)
end
function ns_per_eval(f, F, args; n=200_000)
  s = 0.0
  f(F, args...)
  t = @elapsed for i in 1:n
    s += first(Tuple(f(F + (1e-12 * i) * one(F), args...)))
  end
  1e9 * t / n, s
end
println("\nmodel                 isbits(model)  isbits(P)  alloc P  alloc A   ns/P    ns/A")
for m in models
  _, Pm, Am = m.model()
  aP, aA = allocations(Pm, Fs.large, m.args), allocations(Am, Fs.large, m.args)
  tP, _ = ns_per_eval(Pm, Fs.large, m.args)
  tA, _ = ns_per_eval(Am, Fs.large, m.args)
  @printf("%-20s  %-13s  %-9s  %7d  %7d  %6.1f  %6.1f\n", m.name, isbits(m.model), isbits(Pm), aP, aA, tP, tA)
end

Going further

  • SecondPiola(model) gives $\Psi$, $\boldsymbol S$ and $\partial\boldsymbol S/\partial\boldsymbol C$ as functions of $\boldsymbol C = \boldsymbol F^T\boldsymbol F$ for the models that implement it (IsochoricNeoHookean3D in 0.3.0).
  • Coupled models follow the same pattern with more arguments: ElectroMechModel returns derivatives with respect to $(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol E)$ (tutorials 04 and 05), MagnetoMechModel with respect to $(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol{\mathcal H})$ (tutorials 08 and 09), and the viscous models take the previous deformation and an internal variable (tutorial 16).
  • Put the user model in a simulation: replace NeoHookean3D by Gent3D(μ=1.0, Jm=20.0, λ=100.0) in tutorial 02; for a homogeneous bar the force–stretch curve is the uniaxial column above.
