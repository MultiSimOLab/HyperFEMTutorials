Tutorial 17: Thermo-mechanics: transient heat conduction and thermal expansion

Source · Notebook · nbviewer

One end of a hyperelastic bar is suddenly heated while the other is kept at the initial temperature. Heat diffuses along the bar and the bar expands, freely: it is held only as much as needed to remove its rigid motions. The temperature has a classical series solution, and the expansion of a free body has two exact properties (the change of length is the integral of the thermal strain, and a linear temperature profile leaves the body free of stress), so every part of the coupled computation can be checked.

Measured on a Mac (M5 Max, Julia 1.13.0, 4 threads, 4 BLAS threads; HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8; compiled backend), whole run including compilation: FAST (752 dofs, 143 time steps) 46 s, full (4 503 dofs, 210 time steps) 49 s; median time per step after compilation 3.9 ms (FAST) and 26 ms (full).

What you will learn

  • the thermo-mechanical models of HyperFEM: ThermalModel, a ThermalLaw and ThermoMechModel, and the derivatives they return as functions of $(\boldsymbol F, \vartheta)$;
  • the weak form of a coupled displacement–temperature problem written term by term from the derivatives of the free energy: mechanical equilibrium, heat conduction, the coupling and the heat capacity;
  • a first-order transient problem in TransientModel: a mass form on one field only (heat capacity), quasi-static mechanics, the BDF(2) scheme and time steps controlled by the error (AdaptiveStepping) with exact output times (tstops);
  • how to restrain a free body with symmetry planes and a point, and how to check the compiled backend (work_summary, counters) in a time loop.

Problem statement

The unknowns are the displacement $\boldsymbol u$ and the temperature increment $\vartheta = \theta - \theta_r$ over the reference temperature $\theta_r$. With the Helmholtz free energy $\Psi(\boldsymbol F, \vartheta)$, the first Piola stress is $\boldsymbol P = \partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi$, and the heat flux follows Fourier's law in the reference configuration, $\boldsymbol Q = -k\nabla_0\vartheta$. Neglecting inertia and the heat produced by the deformation (the thermo-elastic heating $-\theta\,\partial^2_{\boldsymbol F\vartheta}\Psi:\dot{\boldsymbol F}$: for a free body it is $9K\alpha_{th}^2\theta_r\,\dot\vartheta$, a fraction $9K\alpha_{th}^2\theta_r/c_v \approx 5.7\cdot10^{-5}$ of the heat-capacity term with the data below, whatever the amplitude; see «Going further»), the problem reads: find $(\boldsymbol u, \vartheta)$ such that

\[\int_\Omega \boldsymbol P(\boldsymbol F, \vartheta) : \nabla_0 \boldsymbol v \,\mathrm dV = 0 , \qquad \int_\Omega c_v\,\dot\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta \,\mathrm dV + \int_\Omega k\,\nabla_0\vartheta\cdot\nabla_0\delta\vartheta \,\mathrm dV = 0 \qquad \forall (\boldsymbol v, \delta\vartheta) ,\]

with $\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I + \nabla_0\boldsymbol u$, the heat capacity per unit volume $c_v$ and the conductivity $k$. The free energy of ThermoMechModel is

\[\Psi(\boldsymbol F, \vartheta) = f(\theta)\,\Psi_m(\boldsymbol F) + \Psi_t(\vartheta) - 3\alpha\,(J^2 - 1)\,\vartheta , \qquad J = \det\boldsymbol F ,\]

with the neo-Hookean $\Psi_m = \tfrac{\mu}{2}(\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F - 3) - \mu\ln J + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}(J-1)^2$, the purely thermal $\Psi_t(\vartheta) = c_v\,[\vartheta - \theta\ln(\theta/\theta_r)]$ (whose $-\theta\,\partial^2_{\vartheta\vartheta}\Psi_t = c_v$), the thermal law $f(\theta)$ (here $f = 1$: the stiffness does not depend on the temperature) and the coupling term. Linearising $\boldsymbol P$ at $\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I$, a free body heated uniformly by $\vartheta$ expands with the strain $\varepsilon = 6\alpha\vartheta/(3\lambda + 2\mu)$: the coefficient of linear thermal expansion is $\alpha_{th} = 2\alpha/K$, with the bulk modulus $K = \lambda + 2\mu/3$. We give $\alpha_{th}$ and set $\alpha = K\alpha_{th}/2$.

The bar $\Omega = (0,L)\times(0,W)\times(0,H)$ is a quarter of a bar of section $2W\times 2H$: the planes $y = 0$ and $z = 0$ are symmetry planes (no normal displacement), and the origin cannot move along $x$. These are the minimal restraints: they remove the rigid motions and nothing else. The bar starts at $\vartheta = 0$; the temperature of the end $x = 0$ rises linearly to $\Delta T$ in a short time $t_r$, $\vartheta(0, t) = \Delta T\,g(t)$ with $g(t) = \min(t/t_r, 1)$, the end $x = L$ is kept at $\vartheta = 0$ and the lateral faces are insulated. (A sudden jump of the end temperature, $t_r = 0$, is the textbook case, but neither the mesh nor the error control of the time step can follow it: the step collapses to its minimum on fine meshes. A short ramp is also what a real heater does.) The temperature does not depend on the displacement, so it is that of a 1D rod; by Duhamel's principle,

\[\vartheta(x, t) = \Delta T\,g(t)\Big(1 - \frac{x}{L}\Big) - \sum_{n\ge1} \frac{2\Delta T}{n\pi}\, \frac{1 - e^{-\lambda_n \min(t, t_r)}}{\lambda_n t_r}\, e^{-\lambda_n \max(t - t_r, 0)} \sin\frac{n\pi x}{L} , \qquad \lambda_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2 D}{L^2} , \quad D = \frac{k}{c_v} ,\]

(for $t_r \to 0$ the classical series of the sudden jump), which tends to the linear profile $\Delta T(1 - x/L)$. For the mechanics, two exact results of linear thermo-elasticity for a free body: (i) by the reciprocal theorem (with a uniform uniaxial stress as the second state), the change of length of the bar, averaged over the end faces, is the integral of the thermal strain, $\Delta\bar\ell(t) = \alpha_{th}\int_\Omega \vartheta\,\mathrm dV / A$ ($A = WH$), at every time; (ii) a temperature linear in the coordinates gives a compatible thermal strain, so at steady state the bar is free of stress and $\Delta\bar\ell = \alpha_{th}\Delta T L/2$.

The data are dimensionless for the mechanics and the conduction (any consistent set of units), and the temperature is in kelvin: $L = 1$, $W = H = 0.1$, $\mu = 1$, $\lambda = 1.5$ ($\nu = 0.3$), $c_v = 1$, $k = 1$ ($D = 1$, diffusion time $L^2/(\pi^2 D) \approx 0.1$), $t_r = 0.01$, $\theta_r = 293.15$, $\Delta T = 10$, $\alpha_{th} = 10^{-4}$ (thermal strains of $10^{-3}$ at most: small deformations).

Geometry and mesh

A Cartesian mesh of hexahedra along the bar. The temperature varies along $x$ only, so the section needs few elements. The restraints of the displacement use four tags: the origin (all components fixed through mask below), the edge $y = z = 0$ (on both symmetry planes), and the two symmetry planes. A node that belongs to several tags takes the first of them in the list of conditions, so the most restrained come first.

const L, W, H = 1.0, 0.1, 0.1
partition = FAST ? (16, 1, 1) : (32, 2, 2)
geometry = CartesianDiscreteModel((0, L, 0, W, 0, H), partition)
labels = get_face_labeling(geometry)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "origin", CartesianTags.corner000)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "axis", CartesianTags.edgeX00⁺)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "y0", CartesianTags.faceX0Z⁺)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "z0", CartesianTags.faceXY0⁺)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "hot", CartesianTags.face0YZ⁺)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "cold", CartesianTags.face1YZ⁺)

Constitutive model

ThermalModel holds $c_v$, $\theta_r$, $\alpha$ and $k$; ConstantEnergyLaw() is the law $f = 1$ (EntropicElasticityLaw(θr=θr, γ=0.0), $f = \theta/\theta_r$, would make the stiffness proportional to the absolute temperature, as in rubber). Calling the model returns the energy and its derivatives as functions of $(\boldsymbol F, \vartheta)$: $(\Psi, \partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi, \partial_\vartheta\Psi, \partial^2_{\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol F}\Psi, \partial^2_{\vartheta\vartheta}\Psi, \partial^2_{\boldsymbol F\vartheta}\Psi, \eta)$, with the entropy $\eta = -\partial_\vartheta\Psi$. These functions are all the weak form needs; we compose them below with the kinematics. The mechanical kinematics gives $\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I + \nabla_0\boldsymbol u$ from the displacement gradient (Gridap's ∇(u) is the transpose of $\nabla_0\boldsymbol u$, hence F ∘ ∇(u)'); the temperature enters the functions as it is (no kinematic map: Kinematics(Thermo, Solid) exists but carries no function, so we do not use it).

const μ, λ = 1.0, 1.5
const cv, k = 1.0, 1.0
const θr, ΔT, αth = 293.15, 10.0, 1e-4
const t_r = 0.01                                               # duration of the ramp of the end temperature
const K = λ + 2μ / 3                                           # bulk modulus
const E = μ * (3λ + 2μ) / (λ + μ)                              # Young's modulus
const D = k / cv                                               # thermal diffusivity
physmodel = ThermoMechModel(ThermalModel(Cv=cv, θr=θr, α=K * αth / 2, κ=k), NeoHookean3D(λ=λ, μ=μ),
                            ConstantEnergyLaw())
Ψ, ∂Ψu, ∂Ψθ, ∂Ψuu, ∂Ψθθ, ∂Ψuθ, η = physmodel()
F, _, _ = get_Kinematics(Kinematics(Mechano, Solid))

Boundary conditions

One set per field, gathered by MultiFieldBC. The temperature of the end $x = 0$ is $\Delta T$ times the evolution function EvolutionFunctions.ramp(t_r), $g(t) = \min(t/t_r, 1)$, evaluated by TransientModel at the time of every stage (it is 0 in the initial state, which is therefore consistent with the conditions). The other conditions are constant.

Du = BoundaryConditions(
  BoundaryCondition("origin", [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]),
  BoundaryCondition("axis", [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]; mask=(false, true, true)),
  BoundaryCondition("y0", [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]; mask=(false, true, false)),
  BoundaryCondition("z0", [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]; mask=(false, false, true)))
Dθ = BoundaryConditions(BoundaryCondition("hot", ΔT, EvolutionFunctions.ramp(t_r)), BoundaryCondition("cold", 0.0))
D_bc = MultiFieldBC([Du, Dθ])

Finite element spaces

Quadratic displacements and linear temperatures (Q2/Q1): the thermal strain $\alpha_{th}\vartheta$ and the strain of the displacement are then polynomials of the same degree, and the stress-free state of a linear temperature profile is represented exactly.

reffeu = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, VectorValue{3,Float64}, 2)
reffeθ = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, Float64, 1)
Vu = TestFESpace(geometry, reffeu, D_bc[1], conformity=:H1)
Vθ = TestFESpace(geometry, reffeθ, D_bc[2], conformity=:H1)
Uu = TrialFESpace(Vu, D_bc[1], 0.0)
Uθ = TrialFESpace(Vθ, D_bc[2], 0.0)
V = MultiFieldFESpace([Vu, Vθ])
U = MultiFieldFESpace([Uu, Uθ])
Ω = Triangulation(geometry)
dΩ = Measure(Ω, 4)
println("free dofs: ", num_free_dofs(U), " (u: ", num_free_dofs(Uu), ", ϑ: ", num_free_dofs(Uθ), ")")

Weak form

We write the residual and its tangent term by term from the derivatives of the free energy; every term is a Gridap integral of the model's functions composed (∘) with the kinematics at the trial functions. ⊙ contracts two second-order tensors ($\boldsymbol A:\boldsymbol B$) and ⋅² the last two indices of a fourth-order tensor with a second-order one, $(\mathbb C\cdot^2\boldsymbol A)_{ij} = C_{ijkl}A_{kl}$.

Mechanical equilibrium. The stress is the derivative of the free energy at the current temperature,

\[R_u = \int_\Omega \boldsymbol P(\boldsymbol F, \vartheta) : \nabla_0\boldsymbol v \,\mathrm dV , \qquad \boldsymbol P = \partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi = \partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi_m - 6\alpha J\vartheta\,\boldsymbol H , \qquad \boldsymbol H = J\boldsymbol F^{-T} ,\]

where the second term is the thermal stress of the coupling energy ($f = 1$).

r_mech(u, θ, v) = ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ (∂Ψu ∘ (F ∘ ∇(u)', θ)))dΩ

Heat conduction. Fourier's law in the reference configuration, $\boldsymbol Q = -k\nabla_0\vartheta$:

\[R_\vartheta = \int_\Omega k\,\nabla_0\vartheta\cdot\nabla_0\delta\vartheta \,\mathrm dV .\]

r_cond(θ, vθ) = ∫(k * (∇(θ) ⋅ ∇(vθ)))dΩ

Tangent. The derivative of $R_u + R_\vartheta$ in the direction $(\delta\boldsymbol u, \delta\vartheta)$ (trial functions du, dθ) has three blocks. The mechanical block, with the elasticity tensor $\mathbb C = \partial^2_{\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol F}\Psi$,

\[K_{uu} = \int_\Omega \nabla_0\boldsymbol v : \mathbb C : \nabla_0\delta\boldsymbol u \,\mathrm dV ;\]

the coupling block, the stress produced by a temperature change, with $\partial^2_{\boldsymbol F\vartheta}\Psi = -6\alpha J\boldsymbol H$,

\[K_{u\vartheta} = \int_\Omega \nabla_0\boldsymbol v : \partial^2_{\boldsymbol F\vartheta}\Psi\;\delta\vartheta \,\mathrm dV ;\]

and the conduction block

\[K_{\vartheta\vartheta} = \int_\Omega k\,\nabla_0\delta\vartheta\cdot\nabla_0\delta\vartheta^{*} \,\mathrm dV\]

($\delta\vartheta^{*}$ the test function vθ). There is no block $K_{\vartheta u}$: the conduction is written in the reference configuration and the thermo-elastic heating is neglected, so the temperature equation does not depend on the displacement.

j_mech(u, θ, du, v) = ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ ((∂Ψuu ∘ (F ∘ ∇(u)', θ)) ⋅² ∇(du)'))dΩ
j_coupling(u, θ, dθ, v) = ∫(∇(v)' ⊙ ((∂Ψuθ ∘ (F ∘ ∇(u)', θ)) * dθ))dΩ
j_cond(dθ, vθ) = ∫(k * (∇(dθ) ⋅ ∇(vθ)))dΩ

Heat capacity. The time derivative enters through the bilinear mass form,

\[M(\dot\vartheta, \delta\vartheta) = \int_\Omega c_v\,\dot\vartheta\,\delta\vartheta \,\mathrm dV , \qquad c_v = -\theta\,\partial^2_{\vartheta\vartheta}\Psi ,\]

with the heat capacity of the model (∂Ψθθ(F, ϑ) = -c_v/θ for ThermalModel and $f = 1$, so $c_v$ is the constant Cv given to it). The form acts on the temperature only: TransientModel assembles it once into a matrix $\boldsymbol M$ and adds $\boldsymbol M\dot{\boldsymbol x}$ (with $\dot{\boldsymbol x}$ given by the time scheme) to the residual of every stage, and its multiple to the tangent. The displacement has no time derivative: the mechanics is quasi-static, in equilibrium with the temperature of every step.

The static forms are functions of the time t, as TransientModel expects; here they do not depend on it.

res(t) = ((u, θ), (v, vθ)) -> r_mech(u, θ, v) + r_cond(θ, vθ)
jac(t) = ((u, θ), (du, dθ), (v, vθ)) -> j_mech(u, θ, du, v) + j_coupling(u, θ, dθ, v) + j_cond(dθ, vθ)
mass = ((du, dθ), (v, vθ)) -> ∫(cv * (dθ * vθ))dΩ
@assert ∂Ψθθ(one(TensorValue{3,3,Float64}), 0.0) ≈ -cv / θr "heat capacity of the model differs from cv"

Solver and computational model

BDF(2), the second-order backward differentiation formula, is a natural scheme for diffusion: implicit, stable for any step, and it damps the fast components of the start. The step is chosen by the local error estimate of the scheme (AdaptiveStepping): small at the start, when the temperature changes quickly near the heated end, and growing as the bar approaches the steady state. The estimate uses only the degrees of freedom with a time derivative (the temperature); atol is in kelvin. The tolerances of the step are tied to the mesh, $\mathrm{tol} = 0.01\,h^2$ (relative to $\Delta T$), so that the error of the time integration stays below that of the mesh, which is of order $h^2$ for linear elements, when the mesh is refined. tstops are the end of the ramp and the times at which we compare with the series solution: the steps are cut to reach them exactly.

backend=:compiled evaluates the forms with the compiled, threaded kernels of HyperFEMKernels; they are built once, and at every stage the model only rebinds them to the new state (no recompilation). The mechanics is nonlinear (finite strains), so the tangent is updated at every Newton iteration (tangent_update=:iteration, the default).

t_check = (t_r, 0.05, 0.2)
t_end = 1.5
h = L / partition[1]
tol_t = 0.01 * h^2
stepping = AdaptiveStepping(; Δt=1e-4, t_end, rtol=tol_t, atol=tol_t * ΔT, tstops=t_check)
nls = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=20, atol=1e-10, rtol=1e-8, verbose=0)
comp_model = TransientModel(res, jac, U, V, D_bc; scheme=BDF(2), mass, nls, backend=:compiled)
xh = get_state(comp_model)                                     # (u, ϑ) after every accepted step
uh, θh = xh

Post-processing

The exact temperature (series above) is a closure: the parameters are captured when it is built, so the function evaluated at the quadrature points reads only its argument and constants. After the ramp the terms are summed until the exponential factor is negligible; during the ramp the terms decay as $n^{-3}$, and 4 000 of them leave a truncation error below $10^{-6}\Delta T$.

function θ_exact(t)
  X -> begin
    x = X[1]
    s = ΔT * min(t / t_r, 1.0) * (1 - x / L)
    for n in 1:4000
      λn = (n * π / L)^2 * D
      e = exp(-λn * max(t - t_r, 0.0))
      e < 1e-16 && break
      s -= 2ΔT / (n * π) * (1 - exp(-λn * min(t, t_r))) / (λn * t_r) * e * sin(n * π * x / L)
    end
    s
  end
end

After every accepted step we store the time, the change of length (mean $u_x$ on the end faces) and its estimate from the reciprocal theorem; at the check times also the error of the temperature, $\lVert\vartheta_h - \vartheta\rVert_{L^2} / (\Delta T\,|\Omega|^{1/2})$, and the VTK files.

Γ0 = BoundaryTriangulation(geometry, tags="hot")
Γ1 = BoundaryTriangulation(geometry, tags="cold")
dΓ0, dΓ1 = Measure(Γ0, 4), Measure(Γ1, 4)
const A = W * H
volΩ = sum(∫(1.0)dΩ)
ex = VectorValue(1.0, 0.0, 0.0)
elongation(uh) = (sum(∫(uh ⋅ ex)dΓ1) - sum(∫(uh ⋅ ex)dΓ0)) / A
elongation_thermal(θh) = αth * sum(∫(θh)dΩ) / A
θ_error(θh, t) = sqrt(sum(∫(abs2 ∘ (θh - θ_exact(t)))dΩ) / volΩ) / ΔT
stress_norm(uh, θh) = sqrt(sum(∫((∂Ψu ∘ (F ∘ ∇(uh)', θh)) ⊙ (∂Ψu ∘ (F ∘ ∇(uh)', θh)))dΩ) / volΩ)

times, Δℓ, Δℓ_th, wall = Float64[], Float64[], Float64[], Float64[]
checks = Dict{Float64,NamedTuple}()
x_005 = zeros(num_free_dofs(U))                                # state at t = 0.05 (for the check of the tangent)
work_2 = Ref(HyperFEMKernels.counters())                       # kernel counters after the second step
time_post = Ref(0.0)                                           # time spent in the post-processing
function record(post)
  time_post[] += @elapsed begin
    t = post.Λ[post.iter]
    push!(wall, time())                                        # wall clock, for the time per step
    post.iter == 2 && (work_2[] = HyperFEMKernels.counters())
    push!(times, t); push!(Δℓ, elongation(uh)); push!(Δℓ_th, elongation_thermal(θh))
    tc = findfirst(s -> isapprox(t, s; atol=1e-12), (t_check..., t_end))
    if tc !== nothing
      checks[t] = (err=θ_error(θh, t), σ=stress_norm(uh, θh), Δℓ=Δℓ[end], Δℓ_th=Δℓ_th[end])
      t == 0.05 && (x_005 .= get_free_dof_values(xh))
      pvd = get_pvd(post)
      pvd[t] = createvtk(Ω, joinpath(outdir, "step_$(post.iter).vtu"), cellfields=["u" => uh, "theta" => θh], order=2)
    end
  end
end
post = PostProcessor(comp_model, record; is_vtk=true, filepath=outdir)

Solve

Most of the run time is compilation: of the compiled kernels when the model is built, of the first Newton iteration, and of the Gridap integrals and the VTK output of the post-processing the first time they run. The median time per step is what a step costs after that: the coupled mechanics and conduction solved together, in one or two Newton iterations (the mechanics is almost linear here). The step stays small during the ramp and grows to about $0.1$ near the steady state.

HyperFEMKernels.reset_counters!()
time_solve = @elapsed (x, flag) = solve!(comp_model; stepping, post)
@assert flag == 1 "the time loop did not reach t_end"
work = HyperFEMKernels.counters()
nsteps = length(times)
Δts = diff([0.0; times])
step_times = sort(diff(wall))                                  # wall time between accepted steps
println(@sprintf("%d steps, Δt ∈ [%.2e, %.2e]; solve %.1f s (post-processing %.1f s); median time per step %.1f ms",
                 nsteps, minimum(Δts), maximum(Δts), time_solve, time_post[], 1e3 * step_times[cld(end, 2)]))

Efficiency

What the compiled operators do (work_summary): every group of the stage forms and of the mass form takes a fast path (:linear_maps) and nothing falls back to Gridap. The kernels are generated when the model is built; the counters, reset before solve!, show that the time loop rebinds them to the new state at every attempt (update_fast). The moving Dirichlet values of the ramp need one more kernel (their contribution to the inertia term), generated once in the first step (codegen, update_full); after that nothing is generated or rebuilt: the counters after the second step and at the end agree. HyperFEM 0.3.0 has no accessor for the operators of a TransientModel: the two lines below read internal fields, and may change.

fallbacks = 0
for (what, o) in (("stage forms", comp_model.caches.op.inner), ("mass form", comp_model.caches.opM))
  ws = HyperFEMKernels.work_summary(o)
  global fallbacks += ws.fallback_residual + ws.fallback_jacobian
  println(what, ": ", [(g.kind, g.domain, g.path) for g in ws.groups], "; Gridap fallbacks: ",
          ws.fallback_residual + ws.fallback_jacobian)
end
println("counters after the second step: ", work_2[])
println("counters of the solve:          ", work)
@assert fallbacks == 0 && work.fallback_assemblies == 0 "a form fell back to Gridap"
@assert work.codegen == work_2[].codegen && work.update_full == work_2[].update_full "kernels rebuilt during the time loop"

Checks

The values at the check times: error of the temperature, stress, change of length and its estimate.

println("\n   t        error ϑ                ‖P‖/(E αth ΔT)   Δℓ            Δℓ (reciprocal)")
for t in sort(collect(keys(checks)))
  c = checks[t]
  println(@sprintf("%6.3f   %.3e (%.2f h²)   %.3e        %.6e   %.6e", t, c.err, c.err / h^2, c.σ / (E * αth * ΔT), c.Δℓ, c.Δℓ_th))
end
@assert all(t -> haskey(checks, t), (t_check..., t_end)) "a check time was not reached exactly"

The tangent written above must be the derivative of the residual written above: Newton converges (more slowly) even with a wrong tangent, so the solution alone does not test it. We compare the tangent applied to a direction $\boldsymbol d$ with a central difference of the residual, $[\boldsymbol r(\boldsymbol x + \epsilon\boldsymbol d) - \boldsymbol r(\boldsymbol x - \epsilon\boldsymbol d)]/(2\epsilon)$, at the state of $t = 0.05$ (stressed, with a curved temperature profile). The direction moves every displacement and temperature dof, in proportion to its value with a varying factor, so that all the blocks, the coupling included, take part; the difference is $O(\epsilon^2)$ plus round-off ($10^{-10}$). The rows of the two equations are compared apart: the conduction rows are much larger than the mechanical ones, which carry the coupling block (a sign error in $K_{u\vartheta}$ gives a difference of order one there). The vectors and the matrix are assembled by Gridap from the same res and jac.

r_at(x) = assemble_vector(y -> res(0.05)(FEFunction(U, x), y), V)
d = x_005 .* (1 .+ 0.5 .* sin.(1:length(x_005)))
ϵ = 1e-6
Jd = assemble_matrix((dx, y) -> jac(0.05)(FEFunction(U, x_005), dx, y), U, V) * d
fd = (r_at(x_005 .+ ϵ .* d) .- r_at(x_005 .- ϵ .* d)) ./ (2ϵ)
nu = num_free_dofs(Uu)                                         # the displacement dofs come first
blockdiff(rows) = sqrt(sum(abs2, fd[rows] .- Jd[rows]) / sum(abs2, Jd[rows]))
tangent_error = max(blockdiff(1:nu), blockdiff(nu+1:length(d)))   # mechanical and thermal rows apart
println(@sprintf("tangent against finite differences of the residual: relative difference %.2e (u rows), %.2e (ϑ rows)",
                 blockdiff(1:nu), blockdiff(nu+1:length(d))))
@assert tangent_error < 1e-6 "the tangent is not the derivative of the residual ($tangent_error)"

(a) The temperature against the series solution. The error comes from the mesh (linear elements, $h = L/n_x$: of order $h^2$) and from the time integration (whose tolerance is $0.01h^2$). Measured with $n_x = 16, 32, 64, 128$ (the FAST and full meshes, and two refinements of the full one), the errors are $1.9h^2$ at $t = t_r = 0.01$ (the mesh dominates: the steep profile near the heated end), and $0.30$–$0.41\,h^2$ and $0.14$–$0.32\,h^2$ at $t = 0.05$ and $0.2$, where the time error, made of more steps on finer meshes, adds a part that grows slowly with $n_x$. The tolerances, $3h^2$, $0.8h^2$ and $0.6h^2$, hold for those four meshes with a margin of at least 1.5. At $t = 1.5$ the series has decayed to $10^{-6}\Delta T$, and the linear profile is represented exactly by the linear elements.

for (t, c) in zip(t_check, (3.0, 0.8, 0.6))
  @assert checks[t].err < c * h^2 "temperature at t = $t away from the series solution (error $(checks[t].err))"
end
@assert checks[t_end].err < 1e-5 "temperature at t = $t_end away from the steady linear profile"

(b) The mechanics. The change of length matches the integral of the thermal strain at every step, and at steady state the linear estimate $\alpha_{th}\Delta T L/2$, with the bar free of stress: the stress is $10^{-5}$ of the thermal stress scale $E\alpha_{th}\Delta T$ against $10^{-3}$–$4\cdot10^{-2}$ during the transient. The remaining differences ($2\cdot10^{-3}$ relative) are the second-order terms of the finite-strain model, of relative size $\alpha_{th}\Delta T = 10^{-3}$: with $\Delta T = 1$ they drop tenfold (measured), so they are not a discretisation error.

rel_recip = maximum(abs.(Δℓ .- Δℓ_th) ./ Δℓ_th)
rel_steady = abs(Δℓ[end] / (αth * ΔT * L / 2) - 1)
σ_steady = checks[t_end].σ / (E * αth * ΔT)
println(@sprintf("reciprocal theorem: max relative difference %.2e; steady state: Δℓ/(αth ΔT L/2) - 1 = %.2e, ‖P‖/(E αth ΔT) = %.2e",
                 rel_recip, rel_steady, σ_steady))
@assert rel_recip < 5 * αth * ΔT "change of length away from the integral of the thermal strain ($rel_recip)"
@assert rel_steady < 5 * αth * ΔT "steady change of length away from αth ΔT L/2 ($rel_steady)"
@assert σ_steady < 1e-4 "the bar is not free of stress at steady state ($σ_steady)"
@assert checks[t_check[1]].σ / (E * αth * ΔT) > 1e-2 "no thermal stress during the transient"

Going further

  • The thermo-elastic heating: the energy balance $\theta\dot\eta + \nabla_0\cdot\boldsymbol Q = 0$ with $\eta = -\partial_\vartheta\Psi$ adds $-\theta\,\partial^2_{\boldsymbol F\vartheta}\Psi:\dot{\boldsymbol F}$ to the heat equation; it needs the general level of TransientModel (∂t(ctx, u) on the displacement).
  • EntropicElasticityLaw(θr=θr, γ=0.0) makes the shear stiffness proportional to $\theta$, as in rubber; ThermoMech_Bonet and ThermoMech_EntropicPolyconvex are the other thermo-mechanical models.
  • Clamp the end $x = 0$ (all components): the bar can no longer expand freely, and the thermal stress appears.
