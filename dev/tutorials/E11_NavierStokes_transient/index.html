Tutorial 11: Transient Navier–Stokes flow around a cylinder

Source · Notebook · nbviewer

The flow around a cylinder in a channel is the classical benchmark of incompressible flow solvers (Schäfer & Turek 1996). At a Reynolds number of 20 the flow is steady, with two attached vortices behind the cylinder; at 100 the wake becomes unstable and sheds vortices periodically (the von Kármán street). We march the Navier–Stokes equations in time with a second-order BDF scheme on the mesh of tutorial 10, reach the steady state of the case 2D-1 and compare the drag coefficient and the pressure drop with the published values; the full run then computes the periodic case 2D-2.

Measured on a Mac (M5 Max, 4 Julia threads, 4 BLAS threads, Julia 1.13.0, HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8): 10 972 dofs; FAST (case 2D-1, 12 time steps) 67 s and full (2D-1 with both backends, then 2D-2 with 800 steps) 145 s, including compilation and package loading. A step of 2D-2 (assembly and LU) takes 0.037 s; one Jacobian assembly takes 5.6 ms with the compiled backend and 58 ms with Gridap's.

What you will learn

  • TransientModel at its general level: the residual and the tangent are written with the time context of the step (∂t, δ∂t, the previous states u⁻, u⁻⁻), here with BDF(2);
  • tangent_update=:step: a problem linear in the unknown of the step (Oseen linearisation), assembled and solved once per step;
  • residual-based stabilisation of equal-order elements (SUPG, PSPG, LSIC);
  • TimeStepping, and recording forces at every step with a PostProcessor;
  • assembling with the compiled backend of HyperFEMKernels (backend=:compiled), and checking that it is faster than Gridap's assembly;
  • validation against a published benchmark.

Problem statement

Find the velocity $\boldsymbol u$ and the pressure $p$ such that

\[\rho\left(\partial_t\boldsymbol u + (\nabla\boldsymbol u)\,\boldsymbol u\right) - \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma = \boldsymbol 0 , \qquad \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u = 0 , \qquad \boldsymbol\sigma = -p\boldsymbol I + 2\mu\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\boldsymbol u) ,\]

in the channel of tutorial 10, starting from rest, with the parabolic inflow $\boldsymbol u = (4U_m y(H-y)/H^2, 0)$ ($H = 0.41$ m) imposed from $t = 0$, no slip on the walls and on the cylinder, and $\boldsymbol\sigma\boldsymbol n = \boldsymbol 0$ on the outlet (the natural condition of the symmetric form below). $\rho = 1$ kg/m³, $\mu = 10^{-3}$ Pa s, and the Reynolds number is $Re = \bar U D/\nu$ with the mean inflow velocity $\bar U = 2U_m/3$ and the cylinder diameter $D = 0.1$ m: $U_m = 0.3$ m/s gives $Re = 20$ (case 2D-1) and $U_m = 1.5$ m/s gives $Re = 100$ (2D-2).

Time discretisation. BDF2 replaces the time derivative at $t_{n+1}$ by $\partial_t\boldsymbol u \approx (3\boldsymbol u - 4\boldsymbol u^n + \boldsymbol u^{n-1})/(2\Delta t)$ (backward Euler, $(\boldsymbol u - \boldsymbol u^n)/\Delta t$, in the first step, which has no $\boldsymbol u^{n-1}$). Linearisation. The advecting velocity of the convective term is extrapolated from the history, $\boldsymbol w = 2\boldsymbol u^n - \boldsymbol u^{n-1}$ ($\boldsymbol u^n$ in the first step): the convective term $(\nabla\boldsymbol u)\boldsymbol w$ becomes linear in the unknown (an Oseen problem), the extrapolation error is $O(\Delta t^2)$ like that of BDF2, and every step is one linear solve.

Stabilisation. Linear velocity and pressure need PSPG (tutorial 10), and convection-dominated flow needs SUPG (streamline upwind Petrov–Galerkin). With the residual of the momentum equation in each element, $\boldsymbol R = \rho\,\partial_t\boldsymbol u + \rho(\nabla\boldsymbol u)\boldsymbol w + \nabla p$ (the viscous term vanishes inside linear elements), the weak form of a step is

\[\begin{aligned} &\int_\Omega \rho\,\partial_t\boldsymbol u\cdot\boldsymbol v + 2\mu\,\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\boldsymbol u):\boldsymbol\varepsilon(\boldsymbol v) + \rho\,(\nabla\boldsymbol u)\boldsymbol w\cdot\boldsymbol v - p\,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol v - q\,\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u \,\mathrm d\Omega \\ &\quad + \sum_K\int_K \tau_{\mathrm{SUPG}}\,(\nabla\boldsymbol v)\boldsymbol w\cdot\boldsymbol R - \tau_{\mathrm{PSPG}}\,\nabla q\cdot\boldsymbol R + \tau_{\mathrm{LSIC}}\,(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol v)(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol u) \,\mathrm d\Omega = 0 , \end{aligned}\]

with $\tau_{\mathrm{SUPG}} = \left((2|\boldsymbol u^n|/h)^2 + 9(4\mu/(\rho h^2))^2\right)^{-1/2}$, $\tau_{\mathrm{PSPG}} = \tau_{\mathrm{SUPG}}/\rho$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{LSIC}} = \rho|\boldsymbol u^n| h/2$ (Tezduyar 1992, DOI 10.1016/S0065-2156(08)70153-4), evaluated with the velocity of the previous step and the element size $h = \sqrt{2|K|}$.

Geometry and mesh

The DFG channel of tutorial 10 (7 402 linear triangles, generator data/models/turek.jl).

model = GmshDiscreteModel(joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "models", "turek2d.msh"))
Ω = Triangulation(model)
dΩ = Measure(Ω, 3)

Boundary conditions and finite element spaces

Equal-order P1–P1. The inflow profile is a function of the position; it has no time function, so it is applied in full from the first step (an impulsive start). The third argument of TrialFESpace is the time at which the Dirichlet values are first evaluated: the initial time, 0 (with a time function on the inflow, as in tutorial 12, the value matters). The two cases of this tutorial have different inflow velocities, so the profile is not a function of constants as in tutorial 10: inflow(Um, H) builds it as a closure, which stores the values of Um and H it was built with (the other way to keep a function evaluated at points free of non-constant globals).

Um = 0.3                                                       # m/s: Re = 20
inflow(Um, H) = x -> VectorValue(4Um * x[2] * (H - x[2]) / H^2, 0.0)
D_bc = MultiFieldBC([
  BoundaryConditions(
    BoundaryCondition("inlet", inflow(Um, H)),
    BoundaryCondition("wall", [0.0, 0.0]),
    BoundaryCondition("cylinder", [0.0, 0.0])),
  NothingBC()])
reffeu = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, VectorValue{2,Float64}, 1)
reffep = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, Float64, 1)
Vu = TestFESpace(model, reffeu, D_bc[1], conformity=:H1)
Vp = TestFESpace(model, reffep, D_bc[2], conformity=:H1)
V = MultiFieldFESpace([Vu, Vp])
U = MultiFieldFESpace([TrialFESpace(Vu, D_bc[1], 0.0), TrialFESpace(Vp, D_bc[2], 0.0)])
println("P1–P1: $(num_free_dofs(U)) dofs")

Weak form

At the general level of TransientModel the residual and the tangent are functions of the time context ctx of the step, which returns the forms. ctx[1] is the context of the velocity field: ∂t(cu, u) is the BDF time derivative $c_0\boldsymbol u - (c_1\boldsymbol u^n - c_2\boldsymbol u^{n-1})$ of the unknown u (backward Euler in the first step), δ∂t(cu, du) its derivative in the direction du ($c_0\,\delta\boldsymbol u$), cu.u⁻ and cu.u⁻⁻ are $\boldsymbol u^n$ and $\boldsymbol u^{n-1}$, and cu.step is the number of the step. The time derivative also enters the SUPG and PSPG terms through the residual $\boldsymbol R$.

τsupg(u, h) = ((2 * norm(u) / h)^2 + 9 * (4μ / (ρ * h^2))^2)^(-1 / 2)
τpspg(u, h) = τsupg(u, h) / ρ
τlsic(u, h) = ρ * norm(u) * h * 0.5
ε(∇u) = 0.5 * (∇u + ∇u')

The advecting velocity $\boldsymbol w$ is written as one expression for every step (the operator of the model is rebuilt every step and must keep its type).

function advecting(cu)
  a, b = cu.step == 1 ? (1.0, 0.0) : (2.0, 1.0)
  a * cu.u⁻ - b * cu.u⁻⁻
end
function res(ctx)
  cu = ctx[1]
  un = cu.u⁻; w = advecting(cu)
  τs = τsupg ∘ (un, he); τp = τpspg ∘ (un, he); τl = τlsic ∘ (un, he)
  ((u, p), (v, q)) -> begin
    ut = ∂t(cu, u)
    Ru = ρ * ut + ρ * (∇(u)' ⋅ w)
    ∫(ρ * (ut ⋅ v))dΩ + ∫(2μ * (ε ∘ (∇(u))) ⊙ (ε ∘ (∇(v))))dΩ + ∫(ρ * ((∇(u)' ⋅ w) ⋅ v))dΩ -
    ∫(p * (∇ ⋅ v))dΩ - ∫(q * (∇ ⋅ u))dΩ +
    ∫(τs * ((∇(v)' ⋅ w) ⋅ Ru))dΩ + ∫(τs * ((∇(v)' ⋅ w) ⋅ ∇(p)))dΩ -
    ∫(τp * (∇(q) ⋅ Ru))dΩ - ∫(τp * (∇(q) ⋅ ∇(p)))dΩ +
    ∫(τl * (∇ ⋅ v) * (∇ ⋅ u))dΩ
  end
end
function jac(ctx)
  cu = ctx[1]
  un = cu.u⁻; w = advecting(cu)
  τs = τsupg ∘ (un, he); τp = τpspg ∘ (un, he); τl = τlsic ∘ (un, he)
  ((u, p), (du, dp), (v, q)) -> begin
    dut = δ∂t(cu, du)
    dRu = ρ * dut + ρ * (∇(du)' ⋅ w)
    ∫(ρ * (dut ⋅ v))dΩ + ∫(2μ * (ε ∘ (∇(du))) ⊙ (ε ∘ (∇(v))))dΩ + ∫(ρ * ((∇(du)' ⋅ w) ⋅ v))dΩ -
    ∫(dp * (∇ ⋅ v))dΩ - ∫(q * (∇ ⋅ du))dΩ +
    ∫(τs * ((∇(v)' ⋅ w) ⋅ dRu))dΩ + ∫(τs * ((∇(v)' ⋅ w) ⋅ ∇(dp)))dΩ -
    ∫(τp * (∇(q) ⋅ dRu))dΩ - ∫(τp * (∇(q) ⋅ ∇(dp)))dΩ +
    ∫(τl * (∇ ⋅ v) * (∇ ⋅ du))dΩ
  end
end

Solver and computational model

The step problem is linear in the unknown, with a tangent that depends on the history (through $\boldsymbol w$ and the $\tau$'s): tangent_update=:step assembles it once per step and solves once from the zero guess, with no Newton iterations. With 11 000 dofs a sparse LU is the right linear solver (tutorial 13 shows the iterative solvers for larger problems).

backend=:compiled assembles the forms with HyperFEMKernels instead of Gridap: the weak form is compiled once into a kernel per element, and at every later step the model only re-captures the forms of the new step (the history fields and the BDF coefficients) without compiling again. The forms are written exactly as for Gridap; backend=:lazy (the default) gives the same solution, assembled by Gridap.

nls = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); verbose=0)
flow = TransientModel(res, jac, U, V, D_bc; scheme=BDF(2), tangent_update=:step, nls=nls, backend=:compiled)

Post-processing

The force on the cylinder, $\boldsymbol F = -\int_{\Gamma_c}\boldsymbol\sigma\boldsymbol n\,\mathrm d\Gamma$ ($\boldsymbol n$ the outward normal of the fluid), gives the drag and lift coefficients $C_{D,L} = 2F_{x,y}/(\rho\bar U^2 D)$; $\Delta p = p(0.15, 0.2) - p(0.25, 0.2)$ is the pressure difference between the front and the back of the cylinder.

Γc = BoundaryTriangulation(model, tags="cylinder")
dΓc = Measure(Γc, 3)
nc = get_normal_vector(Γc)
function coefficients(xh, Ū)
  uh, ph = xh
  σ = 2μ * (ε ∘ (∇(uh))) - ph * I2D
  F = -sum(∫(σ ⋅ nc)dΓc)
  cD, cL = 2 * F[1] / (ρ * Ū^2 * Dc), 2 * F[2] / (ρ * Ū^2 * Dc)
  Δp = ph(Point(0.15, 0.2)) - ph(Point(0.25, 0.2))
  (; cD, cL, Δp)
end

After every step the drag coefficient is stored (post.Λ holds the times of the accepted steps) and the last state is written to VTK.

Ū = 2Um / 3
Δt, nsteps = 0.5, 12
cD_history = Float64[]
function record(post)
  xh = get_state(flow)
  push!(cD_history, coefficients(xh, Ū).cD)
  t = post.Λ[end]
  println("step $(post.iter)  t = $t s  C_D = $(cD_history[end])")
  if post.iter == nsteps
    get_pvd(post)[t] = createvtk(Ω, joinpath(outdir, "re20"), cellfields=["u" => xh[1], "p" => xh[2]])
  end
end
post = PostProcessor(flow, record; is_vtk=true, filepath=outdir)

Solve: case 2D-1 (Re = 20)

The flow starts from rest and reaches the steady state in a few seconds; the time step is large ($\Delta t = 0.5$ s) because only the final state matters here.

HyperFEMKernels.reset_counters!()
t2d1 = @elapsed x, flag = solve!(flow; stepping=TimeStepping(; Δt=Δt, t_end=nsteps * Δt), post=post)
re20 = coefficients(get_state(flow), Ū)
println("2D-1: C_D = $(re20.cD), C_L = $(re20.cL), Δp = $(re20.Δp)  (reference 5.5795, 0.0106, 0.1175)")
println("2D-1: $nsteps steps in $(round(t2d1, digits=2)) s (the first ones include compilation)")

What did the compiled backend do? HyperFEMKernels.work_summary lists the groups of integrals of the operator of the last step and the path each one takes: the Jacobian of this form is :factorised (test factor times trial factor at every point, the fastest path); :duals would mean automatic differentiation with nested dual numbers. HyperFEMKernels.counters() counts the work done since reset_counters!(): update_full the steps whose forms had to be compiled again (one, during the start-up of the scheme: the first step, backward Euler with a single previous state, differs from the BDF2 steps that follow) and update_fast the steps that only re-captured the new history.

ws = HyperFEMKernels.work_summary(flow.caches.op.inner)
foreach(g -> println("  ", g.kind, " group ", g.group, ": ", g.cells, " cells × ", g.points, " points, path ", g.path), ws.groups)
counts = HyperFEMKernels.counters()
println("  Gridap fallbacks: residual $(ws.fallback_residual), Jacobian $(ws.fallback_jacobian); updates: $(counts.update_fast) fast, $(counts.update_full) full")

Checks (2D-1)

Reference values: $C_D = 5.5795$, $C_L = 0.0106$, $\Delta p = 0.1175$ (rounded from the high-order computation of Nabh 1998, Heidelberg preprint 42/98, listed with the FEATFLOW benchmark results; all within the intervals of Schäfer & Turek 1996). The tolerances, 3 % and 2 %, are those of the test of this simulation in HyperFEM; the regression value $\|\boldsymbol x\|$ is that of the same test (HyperFEM 0.3.0, Gridap 0.20.8, Julia 1.13.0, macOS arm64). The lift is not checked: at Re = 20 it is tiny (0.2 % of the drag), and the computed $C_L = -0.037$ differs from 0.0106 by about 1 % of the force on the cylinder, the size of the drag error (−1.8 %); P1–P1 on this mesh does not resolve it.

The compiled backend is checked here in both modes without timings: the Jacobian takes the :factorised path, nothing falls back to Gridap and the forms are not compiled again in the loop. The timing check (next section) runs only in the full run.

@assert flag == 1 "the time loop did not complete"
@assert length(cD_history) == nsteps "a time step was bisected"
@assert isapprox(re20.cD, 5.5795, rtol=0.03) "C_D = $(re20.cD) away from the benchmark value 5.5795"
@assert isapprox(re20.Δp, 0.1175, rtol=0.02) "Δp = $(re20.Δp) away from the benchmark value 0.1175"
@assert abs(cD_history[end] - cD_history[end-1]) < 1e-3 * cD_history[end] "the flow is not steady at t = $(nsteps * Δt) s"
@assert isapprox(norm(x), 13.67687986714, rtol=1e-5) "regression: ‖x‖ = $(norm(x))"
@assert all(g.path === :factorised for g in ws.groups if g.kind === :jacobian) "the compiled Jacobian is not factorised"
@assert ws.fallback_residual == 0 && ws.fallback_jacobian == 0 "part of the forms is assembled by Gridap"
@assert counts.update_full <= 1 "the compiled forms were compiled again during the time loop"

Compiled or Gridap assembly?

The same model with backend=:lazy gives the same solution. We time one assembly of the Jacobian of the last step with each backend, on the same state, after a warm-up call (flow.caches.op is the operator of the step that the solver assembles, flow.caches.x the state). The compiled Jacobian must be at least twice as fast (we measured 5 to 10 times): this check catches a slow pattern in the forms, such as a function evaluated at points that reads a non-constant global (tutorial 10), which turns every evaluation at a point into a dynamic call. With a non-constant $\mu$ inside τsupg we measured 42 ms for the compiled Jacobian instead of 6 ms, against 67 ms for Gridap's.

function jacobian_seconds(m)
  op, xm = m.caches.op, m.caches.x
  A = allocate_jacobian(op, xm)
  Gridap.Algebra.jacobian!(A, op, xm)                          # warm-up (compilation)
  minimum(@elapsed(Gridap.Algebra.jacobian!(A, op, xm)) for _ in 1:5)
end
if !FAST
  Ul = MultiFieldFESpace([TrialFESpace(Vu, D_bc[1], 0.0), TrialFESpace(Vp, D_bc[2], 0.0)])
  flow_lazy = TransientModel(res, jac, Ul, V, D_bc; scheme=BDF(2), tangent_update=:step, nls=nls, backend=:lazy)
  tlazy = @elapsed xl, _ = solve!(flow_lazy; stepping=TimeStepping(; Δt=Δt, t_end=nsteps * Δt))
  tj_compiled, tj_lazy = jacobian_seconds(flow), jacobian_seconds(flow_lazy)
  println("2D-1 with Gridap's assembly: $nsteps steps in $(round(tlazy, digits=2)) s, ‖x - x_lazy‖/‖x‖ = $(norm(x - xl) / norm(x))")
  println("one Jacobian: compiled $(round(1e3 * tj_compiled, digits=2)) ms, Gridap $(round(1e3 * tj_lazy, digits=2)) ms")
  @assert norm(x - xl) < 1e-8 * norm(x) "the two backends give different solutions"
  @assert tj_compiled < tj_lazy / 2 "the compiled Jacobian ($tj_compiled s) is not twice as fast as Gridap's ($tj_lazy s)"
end

Case 2D-2 (Re = 100): vortex shedding

With $U_m = 1.5$ m/s the wake is unstable. The cylinder is slightly below the axis of the channel, so the symmetric start is disturbed from the beginning and the shedding develops by itself: the lift oscillates with its final amplitude after some 3 s. Only the inflow changes: new boundary conditions, spaces and model, with the same res and jac. The time step resolves the shedding period (about 0.33 s) with some 30 steps. We record the force coefficients and the pressure difference at every step, and write the flow every few steps during the last period.

if !FAST
  Um2 = 1.5                                                    # m/s: Re = 100
  Ū2 = 2Um2 / 3
  D2 = MultiFieldBC([
    BoundaryConditions(
      BoundaryCondition("inlet", inflow(Um2, H)),
      BoundaryCondition("wall", [0.0, 0.0]),
      BoundaryCondition("cylinder", [0.0, 0.0])),
    NothingBC()])
  Vu2 = TestFESpace(model, reffeu, D2[1], conformity=:H1)
  Vp2 = TestFESpace(model, reffep, D2[2], conformity=:H1)
  V2 = MultiFieldFESpace([Vu2, Vp2])
  U2 = MultiFieldFESpace([TrialFESpace(Vu2, D2[1], 0.0), TrialFESpace(Vp2, D2[2], 0.0)])
  flow2 = TransientModel(res, jac, U2, V2, D2; scheme=BDF(2), tangent_update=:step, nls=nls, backend=:compiled)

  Δt2, t_end2 = 0.01, 8.0
  ts, cDs, cLs, Δps = Float64[], Float64[], Float64[], Float64[]
  function record2(post)
    xh = get_state(flow2)
    c = coefficients(xh, Ū2)
    t = post.Λ[end]
    push!(ts, t); push!(cDs, c.cD); push!(cLs, c.cL); push!(Δps, c.Δp)
    post.iter % 50 == 0 && println("t = $(round(t, digits=2)) s  C_D = $(c.cD)  C_L = $(c.cL)")
    if t > t_end2 - 0.34 && post.iter % 3 == 0
      get_pvd(post)[t] = createvtk(Ω, joinpath(outdir, "re100_$(post.iter)"), cellfields=["u" => xh[1], "p" => xh[2]])
    end
  end
  post2 = PostProcessor(flow2, record2; is_vtk=true, filepath=outdir)
  t2d2 = @elapsed x2, flag2 = solve!(flow2; stepping=TimeStepping(; Δt=Δt2, t_end=t_end2), post=post2)
  println("2D-2: $(length(ts)) steps in $(round(t2d2, digits=1)) s, $(round(t2d2 / length(ts), digits=3)) s per step")
end

Shedding frequency

The Strouhal number $St = f D/\bar U$ from the period $1/f$ between the upward zero crossings of $C_L - \overline{C_L}$ in the last 3 s, and the maxima of $C_D$ and $C_L$ there. $\Delta p$ is reported, as in the benchmark, half a period after the maximum of $C_L$.

if !FAST
  win = ts .>= t_end2 - 3.0
  tw, cLw, cDw, Δpw = ts[win], cLs[win], cDs[win], Δps[win]
  c = cLw .- sum(cLw) / length(cLw)
  crossings = [tw[i] - c[i] * (tw[i+1] - tw[i]) / (c[i+1] - c[i]) for i in 1:length(c)-1 if c[i] < 0 <= c[i+1]]
  periods = diff(crossings)
  T = sum(periods) / length(periods)
  St = Dc / (Ū2 * T)
  i0 = findlast(t -> t <= tw[end] - T, tw)                     # maximum of C_L in the last full period
  i0 = findmax(cLw[i0-round(Int, T / Δt2):i0])[2] + i0 - round(Int, T / Δt2) - 1
  thalf = tw[i0] + T / 2
  j = findlast(t -> t <= thalf, tw)
  Δp_half = Δpw[j] + (Δpw[j+1] - Δpw[j]) * (thalf - tw[j]) / (tw[j+1] - tw[j])
  println("2D-2 (Re = 100, Δt = $Δt2 s, P1–P1, 7 402 triangles)   reference (Schäfer & Turek 1996)")
  println("  St             = $(round(St, digits=4))       0.2950 – 0.3050")
  println("  max C_D        = $(round(maximum(cDw), digits=4))       3.2200 – 3.2400")
  println("  max C_L        = $(round(maximum(cLw), digits=4))       0.9900 – 1.0100")
  println("  Δp(t₀ + T/2)   = $(round(Δp_half, digits=4))       2.4600 – 2.5000")
  spread = (maximum(periods) - minimum(periods)) / T
  println("  $(length(periods)) periods in the last 3 s, relative spread $(spread), C_L amplitude $((maximum(cLw) - minimum(cLw)) / 2)")
end

Checks (2D-2)

The shedding is periodic (several periods of equal length, a lift that oscillates with a finite amplitude) and its frequency is that of the benchmark: we measured $St = 0.3007$ and assert it within 3 % of 0.30. On this mesh with linear elements the force amplitudes are further from the reference values (we measured $\max C_D = 3.187$ and $\max C_L = 0.952$, 1 % and 4 % below the reference intervals; $\Delta p = 2.475$ is inside its interval): they are only reported.

if !FAST
  @assert flag2 == 1 "the time loop of 2D-2 did not complete"
  @assert length(periods) >= 5 && spread < 0.02 "no periodic vortex shedding (periods $periods)"
  @assert (maximum(cLw) - minimum(cLw)) / 2 > 0.3 "lift amplitude too small for vortex shedding"
  @assert isapprox(St, 0.30, rtol=0.03) "Strouhal number $St away from the benchmark value 0.30"
end

Going further

  • scheme=BDF(1) is the backward Euler method: first order, more numerical damping. Try it on 2D-2 with the same time step: the shedding is weaker or delayed. A time-dependent inflow, such as the case 2D-3 of the benchmark ($U_m = 1.5\sin(\pi t/8)$), is a time function of the inlet condition: BoundaryCondition("inlet", inflow(1.5, H), t -> sin(π * t / 8)).
  • The Oseen linearisation solves one linear system per step. A full Newton method on the convective term (tangent_update=:iteration with the consistent tangent of $\rho(\nabla\boldsymbol u)\boldsymbol u$) converges to the fully implicit BDF2 solution at the cost of several solves per step.
  • Add a non-constant global to τsupg (write μ2 = 1e-3 without const and use it instead of μ): the solution does not change, but the compiled Jacobian becomes about seven times slower and the check above fails.
  • Taylor–Hood P2–P1 (tutorial 10) needs no PSPG and gets closer to the reference values on the same mesh; with quadratic velocities the SUPG residual also contains the viscous term $-\mu\Delta\boldsymbol u$.
  • Tutorial 12 uses the same formulation for the pulsatile blood flow in an aorta, with resistance–capacitance outlet conditions.
  • Reference: M. Schäfer and S. Turek, Benchmark computations of laminar flow around a cylinder, Notes on Numerical Fluid Mechanics 52 (1996) 547–566, DOI 10.1007/978-3-322-89849-4_39.
