Tutorial 03: Fibre-reinforced cylinder under internal pressure

Source · Notebook · nbviewer

A thick-walled tube, clamped at both ends, is inflated by an internal pressure. Its wall is a soft isotropic matrix reinforced by four families of collagen-like fibres (axial, circumferential and two helical ones), the arrangement used to model arteries. Two features of soft-tissue problems appear here: anisotropic models with fibre directions that vary in space, and a pressure that follows the deformed surface, whose weak form and tangent are written by hand.

What you will learn

  • a quarter of a tube from a Cartesian mesh and a polar map, with symmetry planes imposed by mask;
  • summing an isotropic and an anisotropic model (MooneyRivlin3D() + HGO_4Fibers());
  • fibre directions as finite element fields, passed to the model as extra arguments;
  • a follower pressure: its weak form with the cofactor $\boldsymbol H$ and its consistent tangent;
  • checking the setup against the Lamé solution before trusting the nonlinear results.

Measured on a Mac M5 Max (Julia 1.13.0, 4 threads, 4 BLAS threads; HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8; compiled backend), whole run including compilation: full case (triquadratic Q2 elements, mesh 2×12×30, 21 535 free dofs, 20 increments of 4 Newton iterations) 51 s, of which 16 s in solve! (0.8 s per increment, mostly the LU factorisations, 0.16 s each); FAST case (1×8×20, 5 733 free dofs, 4 increments to 20 % of the pressure) 40 s. One assembly of the tangent on the full mesh: 0.016 s compiled, 0.49 s with Gridap's lazy evaluation.

Problem statement

The tube has inner radius $R_i$, outer radius $R_o$ and length $L$, both ends are clamped and the inner surface $\Gamma_i$ carries the pressure $p$ in the deformed configuration. Find the displacement $\boldsymbol u$ such that

\[\int_\Omega \boldsymbol P(\boldsymbol F) : \nabla_0 \boldsymbol v \,\mathrm dV + \Lambda\, p \int_{\Gamma_i} (\boldsymbol H \boldsymbol N)\cdot \boldsymbol v \,\mathrm dA = 0 \quad \forall \boldsymbol v , \qquad \boldsymbol H = \operatorname{cof}\boldsymbol F = J\boldsymbol F^{-T},\]

with the load factor $\Lambda$ going from 0 to 1. The second term is minus the virtual work of the pressure, $+\int_{\gamma_i} p\,\boldsymbol n\cdot\boldsymbol v\,\mathrm da$ (the pressure acts along $-\boldsymbol n$), pulled back to the reference surface with Nanson's formula $\boldsymbol n\,\mathrm da = \boldsymbol H\boldsymbol N\,\mathrm dA$; $\boldsymbol N$ is the outward normal of the solid (it points to the axis on $\Gamma_i$). Because $\boldsymbol H$ depends on $\boldsymbol u$, the load contributes to the tangent: with $D\boldsymbol H[\delta\boldsymbol u] = \boldsymbol F \times \nabla_0\delta\boldsymbol u$ (the tensor cross product of Bonet, Gil and Ortigosa, × in HyperFEM),

\[\Lambda\, p \int_{\Gamma_i} \big((\boldsymbol F\times\nabla_0\delta\boldsymbol u)\,\boldsymbol N\big)\cdot\boldsymbol v \,\mathrm dA .\]

The strain energy is a Mooney–Rivlin matrix plus four fibre families of Holzapfel–Gasser–Ogden type (Holzapfel, Gasser and Ogden 2000, doi:10.1023/A:1010835316564),

\[\Psi = \frac{\mu_1}{2}\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F + \frac{\mu_2}{2}\boldsymbol H:\boldsymbol H - (\mu_1+2\mu_2)\ln J + \frac{\lambda}{2}(J-1)^2 - \frac{3}{2}(\mu_1+\mu_2) + \sum_{a=1}^{4} \frac{c_{1a}}{4c_{2a}}\left(e^{c_{2a}(I_{4a}-1)^2} - 1\right), \qquad I_{4a} = |\boldsymbol F\boldsymbol M_a|^2 ,\]

with unit fibre directions $\boldsymbol M_a$: axial $\boldsymbol e_z$, circumferential $\boldsymbol e_\theta$ and the two helices $(\boldsymbol e_\theta \pm \boldsymbol e_z)/\sqrt 2$. Units: cm and Pa.

Setup

using HyperFEM
using Gridap
using HyperFEMKernels                                          # compiled backend (`backend=:compiled`)

const FAST = get(ENV, "HYPERFEM_TUTORIALS_FAST", "0") == "1"   # reduced case for the test suite
outdir = joinpath(@__DIR__, "..", "data", "sims", "E03_Fibre_reinforced_cylinder")
setupfolder(outdir)                                            # VTK output (not versioned)

Geometry and mesh

The material and the loads are symmetric with respect to the planes $x = 0$ and $y = 0$ (the two helical families are mirror images of each other), so a quarter of the tube is enough. It is the box $(R_i,R_o)\times(0,\pi/2)\times(0,L)$ in coordinates $(r,\theta,z)$, mapped to Cartesian coordinates by map. The map moves the vertices: the cells stay trilinear, so the cross-section is a polygon, and the quadratic elements below do not curve it.

Ri, Ro, L = 1.0, 1.1, 4.4                                       # cm
polar(X) = Point(X[1] * cos(X[2]), X[1] * sin(X[2]), X[3])
partition = FAST ? (1, 8, 20) : (2, 12, 30)                    # cells along r, θ, z
model = CartesianDiscreteModel((Ri, Ro, 0, π / 2, 0, L), partition; map=polar)

CartesianTags names the faces, edges and corners of the box ($X = r$, $Y = \theta$, $Z = z$). Overlapping tags would leave unclear which mask applies at the shared nodes, so the tags are chosen disjoint: the clamped ends take their edges and corners (⁺), the symmetry planes only their interior and their edges along $z$.

T = CartesianTags
labels = get_face_labeling(model)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "inner", T.face0YZ)                  # r = Ri
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "clamped", [T.faceXY0⁺; T.faceXY1⁺]) # z = 0 and z = L
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "sym_y", [T.faceX0Z; T.edge00Z; T.edge10Z])   # θ = 0, plane y = 0
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "sym_x", [T.faceX1Z; T.edge01Z; T.edge11Z])   # θ = π/2, plane x = 0

Constitutive model

Parameters of the original HyperFEM example (artery-like: $\mu_1+\mu_2$ = 74.4 kPa, nearly incompressible). The first two fibre families (axial, circumferential) are very weak; the helices carry the load. Summing an isotropic and an anisotropic model gives a model whose functions take $\boldsymbol F$ followed by the four fibre directions.

μ1, μ2 = 37.2e3, 37.2e3                                        # Pa
λ = 100 * (μ1 + μ2)
c1 = [4.68e-3, 2.9e-2, 28.16e3, 28.16e3]                       # Pa (axial, circumferential, helix +, helix −)
c2 = [2.97e-7, 1.68e-6, 3.48, 3.48]                            # dimensionless
matrix = MooneyRivlin3D(λ=λ, μ1=μ1, μ2=μ2)
physmodel = matrix + HGO_4Fibers(c1=c1, c2=c2)
kinematics = Kinematics(Mechano, Solid)
F, H, _ = get_Kinematics(kinematics)                           # F(∇u'), H(F) = cof F

Finite element spaces

Q2 elements: the material is nearly incompressible, and trilinear elements lock (they are about 1.5 % too stiff in the Lamé check below on a mesh like this one).

order = 2
reffe = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, VectorValue{3,Float64}, order)
Ω = Triangulation(model)
dΩ = Measure(Ω, 2 * order)
Γi = BoundaryTriangulation(model, tags="inner")
dΓi = Measure(Γi, 2 * order)
N = get_normal_vector(Γi)                                       # reference outward normal on Γi

Fibre fields

The directions are interpolated in a vector space without boundary conditions and passed to the model as finite element functions (the model normalises them at the quadrature points).

eθ(x) = VectorValue(-x[2], x[1], 0.0) / sqrt(x[1]^2 + x[2]^2)
ez(x) = VectorValue(0.0, 0.0, 1.0)
Vf = FESpace(model, reffe)
fibres = (interpolate_everywhere(ez, Vf),                         # M₁: axial
          interpolate_everywhere(eθ, Vf),                         # M₂: circumferential
          interpolate_everywhere(x -> (eθ(x) + ez(x)) / √2, Vf),  # M₃: helix +45°
          interpolate_everywhere(x -> (-eθ(x) + ez(x)) / √2, Vf)) # M₄: helix −45°

Weak form

The weak form of the model comes from residual/jacobian, with the fibres after the load factor; the pressure terms are written as in the equations above. ⋅ between a tensor and a vector is the matrix–vector product.

p = 5e3                                                         # Pa (≈ 37.5 mmHg)
p_final = FAST ? 0.2 * p : p
pressure_res(u, v, pΛ) = pΛ * ∫(((H ∘ (F ∘ ∇(u)')) ⋅ N) ⋅ v)dΓi
pressure_jac(u, du, v, pΛ) = pΛ * ∫((((F ∘ ∇(u)') × ∇(du)') ⋅ N) ⋅ v)dΓi
res(Λ) = (u, v) -> residual(physmodel, kinematics, u, v, dΩ, Λ, fibres...) + pressure_res(u, v, Λ * p_final)
jac(Λ) = (u, du, v) -> jacobian(physmodel, kinematics, u, du, v, dΩ, Λ, fibres...) + pressure_jac(u, du, v, Λ * p_final)

Points where the solution is read

Three points of the inner surface at mid-length, at $\theta = 0, \pi/4, \pi/2$. A finite element function can be evaluated at a vector of points (one search for all of them).

θs = [0.0, π / 4, π / 2]
probe = [polar(Point(Ri, θ, L / 2)) for θ in θs]
er(θ) = VectorValue(cos(θ), sin(θ), 0.0)
radial(uh) = [u ⋅ er(θ) for (u, θ) in zip(uh(probe), θs)]

A check first: the isotropic tube at a small pressure

With the matrix alone, a small pressure and ends that can slide radially but not axially, the solution is Lamé's plane-strain solution $u_r = A r + B/r$, where

\[B = \frac{p}{2\mu\,(R_i^{-2} - R_o^{-2})}, \qquad A = \frac{\mu B}{(\lambda_L+\mu) R_o^2}, \qquad \mu = \mu_1+\mu_2, \qquad \lambda_L = \lambda + 2\mu_2 ,\]

with the Lamé constants of the Mooney–Rivlin energy linearised at $\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I$. It checks the mesh, the symmetry conditions and the pressure term in a few seconds. The sliding ends need $u_z = 0$ and, on the edges they share with the symmetry planes, the symmetry component as well: three more tags.

add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "ends", [T.faceXY0; T.faceXY1; T.edge0Y0; T.edge1Y0; T.edge0Y1; T.edge1Y1])
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "ends_y", [T.edgeX00⁺; T.edgeX01⁺])
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "ends_x", [T.edgeX10⁺; T.edgeX11⁺])
zero3 = [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]
D_lame = BoundaryConditions(
  BoundaryCondition("ends", zero3; mask=(false, false, true)),
  BoundaryCondition("ends_y", zero3; mask=(false, true, true)),
  BoundaryCondition("ends_x", zero3; mask=(true, false, true)),
  BoundaryCondition("sym_y", zero3; mask=(false, true, false)),
  BoundaryCondition("sym_x", zero3; mask=(true, false, false)))
V_lame = TestFESpace(Ω, reffe, D_lame, conformity=:H1)
U_lame = TrialFESpace(V_lame, D_lame)
p_small = 50.0                                                  # Pa: strains of 0.2 %
res_lame(Λ) = (u, v) -> residual(matrix, kinematics, u, v, dΩ) + pressure_res(u, v, Λ * p_small)
jac_lame(Λ) = (u, du, v) -> jacobian(matrix, kinematics, u, du, v, dΩ) + pressure_jac(u, du, v, Λ * p_small)
lame_model = StaticNonlinearModel(res_lame, jac_lame, U_lame, V_lame, D_lame;
  nls=NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=10, rtol=1e-10, verbose=false), backend=:compiled)
_, flag_lame = solve!(lame_model; stepping=LoadStepping(nsteps=1))
μL, λL = μ1 + μ2, λ + 2μ2
B = p_small / (2μL * (1 / Ri^2 - 1 / Ro^2))
A = μL * B / ((λL + μL) * Ro^2)
ur_lame = radial(get_state(lame_model))
println("Lamé check: u_r(Ri) = ", ur_lame[2], " cm, exact ", A * Ri + B / Ri, " cm")

Boundary conditions of the clamped tube

D_bc = BoundaryConditions(
  BoundaryCondition("clamped", zero3),
  BoundaryCondition("sym_y", zero3; mask=(false, true, false)),
  BoundaryCondition("sym_x", zero3; mask=(true, false, false)))
V = TestFESpace(Ω, reffe, D_bc, conformity=:H1)
U = TrialFESpace(V, D_bc)
println("free dofs: ", num_free_dofs(V))

Solver and computational model

backend=:compiled (tutorial 01) compiles the residual and the tangent, the pressure terms included, once; at every increment the model only rebinds them to the new load factor, without compiling again.

nls = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=20, rtol=1e-10, verbose=true)
comp_model = StaticNonlinearModel(res, jac, U, V, D_bc; nls=nls, backend=:compiled)
uh = get_state(comp_model)

Post-processing

After every converged increment: the pressure and the radial displacement at the three probes, and the displacement and fibre fields in a VTK file.

pressures, ur_history = Float64[], Vector{Float64}[]
function record(post)
  Λ = post.Λ[post.iter]
  push!(pressures, Λ * p_final)
  push!(ur_history, radial(uh))
  pvd = get_pvd(post)
  pvd[Λ] = createvtk(Ω, joinpath(outdir, "step_$(post.iter).vtu"),
    cellfields=["u" => uh, "M_axial" => fibres[1], "M_circ" => fibres[2], "M_helix+" => fibres[3], "M_helix-" => fibres[4]])
end
post = PostProcessor(comp_model, record; is_vtk=true, filepath=outdir)

Solve

The pressure is applied in equal increments; an increment whose Newton iteration fails is halved (up to five times in a row). The load does not enter through Dirichlet values, so ProjectDirichlet is not needed here.

nsteps = FAST ? 4 : 20
x, flag = solve!(comp_model; stepping=LoadStepping(nsteps=nsteps, maxbisec=5), post=post)   # also closes the .pvd
for (pk, ur) in zip(pressures, ur_history)
  println("p = $(round(pk, digits=1)) Pa   u_r(Ri, L/2) = $(round(ur[2], sigdigits=10)) cm")
end

Checks

(a) The isotropic tube matches Lamé's solution within 0.5 % (measured: 0.13 % FAST, 0.19 % full; the rest is discretisation and the small geometric nonlinearity; trilinear elements, about 1.5 % too stiff, would fail). (b) The discrete problem is invariant under rotations about the axis, so the radial displacement is the same at the three probes and there is no twist. (c) A regression value of the final radial displacement (HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8, Julia 1.13.0, macOS arm64).

@assert flag_lame == 1 && flag == 1 "the load stepping did not reach Λ = 1"
@assert abs(ur_lame[2] / (A * Ri + B / Ri) - 1) < 0.005 "isotropic tube away from Lamé's solution"
ur_end = ur_history[end]
@assert maximum(abs.(ur_end .- ur_end[2])) < 1e-8 * abs(ur_end[2]) "radial displacement not axisymmetric"
u45 = uh(probe[2])
@assert abs(u45 ⋅ VectorValue(-sin(π / 4), cos(π / 4), 0.0)) < 1e-8 * abs(ur_end[2]) "the tube twists"
ur_ref = FAST ? 0.0393139021903048 : 0.260654941449461                        # cm, regression values
@assert isapprox(ur_end[2], ur_ref; rtol=1e-5) "radial displacement $(ur_end[2]) ≠ regression value $ur_ref"

Going further

  • Change the helix angle (here ±45°) or remove the helices: the tube becomes softer and, with unbalanced families, twists (the quarter model then no longer applies: mesh the whole tube with (0, 2π) in the map and remove the symmetry tags).
  • Increase the pressure: the exponential fibres stiffen and the pressure–radius curve turns up.
  • Tutorial 05 uses a single fibre family in an electro-active solid; tutorial 13 replaces LUSolver for larger meshes.
