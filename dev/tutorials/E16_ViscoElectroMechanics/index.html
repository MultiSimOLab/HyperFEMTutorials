Tutorial 16: Visco-electro-mechanics: creep of a dielectric bimorph

Source · Notebook · nbviewer

Soft dielectric elastomers are viscoelastic: under a constant voltage an actuator does not stop where it is when the voltage is reached, but keeps deforming for seconds or minutes as the polymer network relaxes. We model the material with a generalised Maxwell model (an elastic equilibrium branch plus two viscous branches with relaxation times of about 1 s and 10 s), apply a voltage ramp to a bimorph cantilever and hold it: the tip deflection creeps towards the value given by the equilibrium branch alone, which we compute separately. The creep turns out to be much slower than the relaxation times suggest, and we predict by how much.

Measured on a Mac (M5 Max, 4 Julia threads, 4 BLAS threads, Julia 1.13.0, HyperFEM 0.3.0, HyperFEMKernels 0.2.6, Gridap 0.20.8; compiled backend), whole run including compilation: FAST 54 s (1 089 free dofs, 41 time steps, 35 ms per step), full 62 s (3 615 free dofs, 87 time steps, 0.10 s per step; the first step takes 7 s, mostly compilation). In a step of the full case the LU factorisation (17 ms, once per Newton iteration) dominates; one assembly of the tangent takes 4.9 ms compiled and 76 ms with Gridap's lazy evaluation.

What you will learn

  • generalised Maxwell viscoelasticity (GeneralizedMaxwell, ViscousPolyconvex) inside an electro-mechanical model (ElectroMechModel);
  • internal variables stored at the quadrature points (CellState) and updated once per accepted time step;
  • TransientModel with the QuasiStatic scheme: a time loop without inertia, where time enters through the material ($\Delta t$ in the viscous update) and through a time-dependent voltage;
  • graded time steps with TimeStepping(; grow, Δtmax, tstops) to cover two relaxation times in a few steps;
  • checking a viscous computation against its long-time (equilibrium) limit, and checking that the compiled backend is not compiled again at every step.

Problem statement

The plate $\Omega = (0,L)\times(0,W)\times(0,H)$ ($L = 50$ mm, $W = 5$ mm, $H = 1$ mm) is clamped at $x = 0$. It carries two compliant electrodes: one on the bottom face $z = 0$ (grounded) and one on the mid-plane $z = H/2$, whose potential follows a ramp of duration $t_r = 1$ s up to $V_0$ and is then held. The electric field lives in the lower layer only: it squeezes the layer across its thickness and, the material being nearly incompressible, the layer elongates. The upper layer resists, and the plate bends upwards ($+z$).

As in tutorial 04, the unknowns are the displacement $\boldsymbol u$ and the electric potential $\varphi$, with $\boldsymbol F = \boldsymbol I + \nabla_0\boldsymbol u$ and $\boldsymbol E_0 = -\nabla_0\varphi$. The free energy is now the sum of three mechanical branches and the ideal dielectric,

\[\Psi(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol E_0; \boldsymbol C_{v,1}, \boldsymbol C_{v,2}) = \Psi_\infty(\boldsymbol F) + \sum_{a=1}^{2} \Psi_a(\boldsymbol C, \boldsymbol C_{v,a}) - \frac{\varepsilon}{2J}\,|\boldsymbol H \boldsymbol E_0|^2 , \qquad \boldsymbol C = \boldsymbol F^T\boldsymbol F ,\]

with the compressible neo-Hookean equilibrium branch of tutorial 02, $\Psi_\infty = \tfrac{\mu_\infty}{2}(\boldsymbol F:\boldsymbol F - 3) - \mu_\infty \ln J + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}(J-1)^2$, and two viscous (Maxwell) branches, each a neo-Hookean spring in series with a dashpot, written in terms of a viscous strain $\boldsymbol C_{v,a}$ ($\det\boldsymbol C_{v,a} = 1$):

\[\Psi_a = \frac{\mu_a}{2}\left(\boldsymbol C : \boldsymbol C_{v,a}^{-1} - 3 J^{2/3}\right) .\]

The viscous strains are internal variables: they are not unknowns of the finite element problem but evolve at every quadrature point by a flow rule with relaxation time $\tau_a$, which drives $\boldsymbol C_{v,a}$ towards the isochoric part of $\boldsymbol C$. Integrated with a backward-Euler step from $t_n$ to $t_{n+1} = t_n + \Delta t$ it has a closed form (no local Newton iteration):

\[\boldsymbol C_{v,a}^{-1}\big|_{n+1} = \frac{\boldsymbol B}{(\det\boldsymbol B)^{1/3}} , \qquad \boldsymbol B = \frac{\Delta t}{\tau_a}\,\boldsymbol C_{n+1}^{-1} + \boldsymbol C_{v,a}^{-1}\big|_{n} .\]

For $\Delta t \ll \tau_a$ the viscous strain does not move and the branch acts as an extra spring of modulus $\mu_a$: the instantaneous response is that of all branches together, $\mu_\infty + \mu_1 + \mu_2$. Under a constant $\boldsymbol C$, $\boldsymbol C_{v,a}$ tends to $J^{-2/3}\boldsymbol C$, the energy and the stress of the branch vanish, and the long-time response is that of the equilibrium branch alone.

Relaxation and creep. $\tau_a$ is the time with which the stress of branch $a$ relaxes under a fixed deformation. Under a fixed load the deformation creeps with other times, the retardation times $\lambda$: in the small-strain (linear) version of the model they are the roots of

\[\mu_\infty \lambda^2 - \left(\mu_\infty(\tau_1 + \tau_2) + \mu_1\tau_1 + \mu_2\tau_2\right)\lambda + \tau_1\tau_2\,(\mu_\infty + \mu_1 + \mu_2) = 0\]

(the zeros of the relaxation modulus $\mu_\infty + \sum_a \mu_a s\tau_a/(1 + s\tau_a)$ in the Laplace variable, at $s = -1/\lambda$). With a soft equilibrium branch they are much longer than the relaxation times: the dashpot of a stiff branch must stretch a long way before the soft branch takes over the load. With the material below, $\lambda_1 = 1.67$ s and $\lambda_2 = 39.1$ s (for $\tau_1 = 0.82$ s and $\tau_2 = 10.7$ s).

The problem at $t_{n+1}$ is the electro-mechanical problem of tutorial 04 with the stress of the step (equilibrium branch, viscous branches with $\boldsymbol C_{v,a}$ given by the update above, and Maxwell stress): find $(\boldsymbol u, \varphi)$ such that for all $(\boldsymbol v, \delta\varphi)$

\[\int_\Omega \nabla_0 \boldsymbol v : \partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi \,\mathrm dV - \int_\Omega \nabla_0 \delta\varphi \cdot \partial_{\boldsymbol E_0}\Psi \,\mathrm dV = 0 ,\]

with $\partial_{\boldsymbol F}\Psi$ evaluated at $(\boldsymbol F_{n+1}, \boldsymbol E_{0,n+1})$ and depending on the internal variables of the previous step, $\boldsymbol C_{v,a}|_n$, through the update. There is no inertia: the time only enters through the dashpots and the voltage (a quasi-static time loop). Once the step has converged, the internal variables are updated, and the next step starts from them.

Material (from the HyperFEM test of this simulation): $\mu_\infty = 14$ kPa, $\lambda = 1$ MPa, $\mu_1 = 56$ kPa with $\tau_1 = 0.82$ s, $\mu_2 = 34$ kPa with $\tau_2 = 10.7$ s. The instantaneous shear modulus is 7.4 times the equilibrium one. The permittivity is $\varepsilon = 1$ in scaled units: the solution depends on $\varepsilon$ and the voltage only through $\varepsilon V_0^2$, so $V_0 = 0.008$ below is $0.008/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ volts for a material of permittivity $\varepsilon$ (about 1.2 kV for an acrylic elastomer with $\varepsilon \approx 4.2\cdot 10^{-11}$ F/m).

Geometry and mesh

A Cartesian mesh of the plate with two layers of elements across the thickness, so that the mid-plane is a mesh plane and can carry the electrode. add_tag_from_vertex_filter! tags the faces, edges and vertices whose vertices all satisfy the filter (the filter captures the height of the plane in a let, rather than reading a global variable). The bimorph bends, and trilinear hexahedra are much too stiff in bending when they are this thin (shear locking): we use triquadratic ones. Even so, the meshes are coarse: the equilibrium tip deflection is 5.45 mm with the FAST mesh and 5.77 mm with the full one (6 % apart; 5.9 mm, from a static run not shown here, with 40×2×4 elements). The checks compare results on the same mesh, so they hold for any of them.

L, W, H = 0.050, 0.005, 0.001                                  # m
partition = FAST ? (10, 1, 2) : (20, 2, 2)
model = CartesianDiscreteModel((0, L, 0, W, 0, H), partition)
labels = get_face_labeling(model)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "fixed", CartesianTags.face0YZ⁺)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "bottom", CartesianTags.faceXY0⁺)
add_tag_from_vertex_filter!(labels, "mid", model, let zm = H / 2; x -> abs(x[3] - zm) < 1e-12 end)
add_tag_from_tags!(labels, "tip", CartesianTags.face1YZ)

Constitutive model

GeneralizedMaxwell(equilibrium, branches...) adds viscous branches to an elastic model; ViscousPolyconvex is the branch above. Calling the electro-mechanical model returns the energy and its derivatives as functions of $(\boldsymbol F, \boldsymbol E_0, \boldsymbol F_n, \boldsymbol A_1, \boldsymbol A_2)$: the deformation gradient of the previous step and one internal variable per branch (here $\boldsymbol A_a = \boldsymbol C_{v,a}|_n$) come after the arguments of tutorial 04. $\boldsymbol F_n$ is part of the interface of the viscous models (it is passed to every branch); the update of ViscousPolyconvex above does not use it. The stress is the algorithmic stress, with the viscous strains given by the update of the step, and the tangent is its consistent derivative.

μ∞, λ = 1.4e4, 1.0e6                                           # Pa
μ₁, τ₁ = 5.6e4, 0.82                                           # Pa, s
μ₂, τ₂ = 3.4e4, 10.7                                           # Pa, s
ε = 1.0                                                        # scaled permittivity
viscoelastic = GeneralizedMaxwell(NeoHookean3D(λ=λ, μ=μ∞), ViscousPolyconvex(μ=μ₁, τ=τ₁), ViscousPolyconvex(μ=μ₂, τ=τ₂))
physmodel = ElectroMechModel(mechano=viscoelastic, electro=IdealDielectric(ε=ε))
km = Kinematics(Mechano, Solid)
ke = Kinematics(Electro, Solid)
k = (km, ke)
F, _, _ = get_Kinematics(km)
E = get_Kinematics(ke)

Boundary conditions

The clamp is constant. The potential of the mid-plane electrode follows EvolutionFunctions.ramp(t_r), $\min(t/t_r, 1)$: in a TransientModel the evolution functions receive the time.

V0 = 0.008                                                     # voltage of the mid-plane electrode (scaled)
t_r = 1.0                                                      # s, duration of the ramp
Du = BoundaryConditions(BoundaryCondition("fixed", [0.0, 0.0, 0.0]))
Dφ = BoundaryConditions(BoundaryCondition("bottom", 0.0), BoundaryCondition("mid", V0, EvolutionFunctions.ramp(t_r)))
D_bc = MultiFieldBC([Du, Dφ])

Finite element spaces

order = 2
reffeu = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, VectorValue{3,Float64}, order)
reffeφ = ReferenceFE(lagrangian, Float64, order)
Vu = TestFESpace(model, reffeu, D_bc[1], conformity=:H1)
Vφ = TestFESpace(model, reffeφ, D_bc[2], conformity=:H1)
V = MultiFieldFESpace([Vu, Vφ])
U = MultiFieldFESpace([TrialFESpace(Vu, D_bc[1], 0.0), TrialFESpace(Vφ, D_bc[2], 0.0)])
Ω = Triangulation(model)
dΩ = Measure(Ω, 2 * order)
println("free dofs: ", num_free_dofs(U), " (u: ", num_free_dofs(U[1]), ", φ: ", num_free_dofs(U[2]), ")")

Internal variables

CellState(physmodel, dΩ) creates one internal variable per viscous branch at every quadrature point, initialised to $\boldsymbol C_{v,a} = \boldsymbol I$ (the plate starts relaxed). With backend=:compiled they are stored as dense arrays that the compiled kernels read directly, and their update runs as a compiled kernel too. A is a tuple with one state per branch.

A = CellState(physmodel, dΩ; backend=:compiled)

Weak form

A TransientModel passes to the forms a time context ctx: the time $t_{n+1}$ (ctx.t), the step (ctx.Δt) and the state of the previous step (ctx.u⁻; ctx[1].u⁻ is its first field, the displacement). With the QuasiStatic scheme the unknown of the step is the field itself, so the forms are those of a static problem with $\boldsymbol F_n$ and the internal variables as extra arguments. HyperFEM's helpers build the terms of tutorial 04 with those extra arguments: the mechanical and the electrical residual, and the mechanical, electrical and coupling blocks of the tangent (residual(physmodel, Mechano, k, ...) etc.). The slot after dΩ is the load factor $\Lambda$ of the static helpers, which the kinematics of this problem ignore; we pass ctx.t there only because the extra arguments $\boldsymbol F_n, \boldsymbol A_1, \boldsymbol A_2$ come after it. The time step $\Delta t$ is not an argument: the model reads it from a reference that the driver sets before every attempt (physics=physmodel below), so a change of $\Delta t$ changes no form.

res(ctx) = ((u, φ), (v, vφ)) -> begin
  Fn = F ∘ ∇(ctx[1].u⁻)'
  residual(physmodel, Mechano, k, (u, φ), v, dΩ, ctx.t, Fn, A...) +
  residual(physmodel, Electro, k, (u, φ), vφ, dΩ, ctx.t, Fn, A...)
end
jac(ctx) = ((u, φ), (du, dφ), (v, vφ)) -> begin
  Fn = F ∘ ∇(ctx[1].u⁻)'
  jacobian(physmodel, Mechano, k, (u, φ), du, v, dΩ, ctx.t, Fn, A...) +
  jacobian(physmodel, Electro, k, (u, φ), dφ, vφ, dΩ, ctx.t, Fn, A...) +
  jacobian(physmodel, ElectroMechano, k, (u, φ), (du, dφ), (v, vφ), dΩ, ctx.t, Fn, A...)
end

The update of the internal variables, called by the driver once per accepted step with the new state xh and the state of the previous step xh⁻ (a failed attempt leaves them untouched). The arguments are those of the energy: $\boldsymbol F_{n+1}$, $\boldsymbol E_{0,n+1}$ and $\boldsymbol F_n$ (the electric field is not used by the update, but it is part of the call: without it, HyperFEM 0.3.0 does nothing and does not complain, and the internal variables keep their initial value, which turns the viscous branches into elastic springs).

internal(xh, xh⁻) = update_state!(physmodel, A, F ∘ ∇(xh[1])', E ∘ ∇(xh[2]), F ∘ ∇(xh⁻[1])'; backend=:compiled)

Solver and computational model

TransientModel with scheme=QuasiStatic() is the time loop of a problem without inertia. physics receives update_time_step!(physics, Δt) before every attempt; internal is the update above. With backend=:compiled the forms are compiled once; at every step they are only rebound to the new previous state and internal variables (we check it at the end).

nls = NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=20, atol=1e-12, rtol=1e-9, verbose=1)
creep = TransientModel(res, jac, U, V, D_bc; scheme=QuasiStatic(), nls=nls, physics=physmodel, internal=internal,
                       backend=:compiled)

Post-processing

After every accepted step: the time, the voltage, the mean deflection $u_z$ of the free end and the wall-clock time (to measure the cost of a step); the VTK files every few steps. The post-processing runs before the update of the internal variables.

Γtip = BoundaryTriangulation(model, tags="tip")
dΓtip = Measure(Γtip, 2 * order)
ez = VectorValue(0.0, 0.0, 1.0)
ramp_voltage = EvolutionFunctions.ramp(t_r)
times, voltage, tip_uz, wall = Float64[], Float64[], Float64[], Float64[]
function record(post)
  t = post.Λ[post.iter]
  xh = get_state(creep)
  push!(times, t)
  push!(voltage, V0 * ramp_voltage(t))
  push!(tip_uz, sum(∫(xh[1] ⋅ ez)dΓtip) / (W * H))
  push!(wall, time())
  if post.iter % 5 == 1
    get_pvd(post)[t] = createvtk(Ω, joinpath(outdir, "creep_$(post.iter).vtu"), cellfields=["u" => xh[1], "φ" => xh[2]])
  end
end
post = PostProcessor(creep, record; is_vtk=true, filepath=outdir)

Solve

The time scales go from the ramp and $\tau_1$ (about a second) to $\lambda_2$ (40 s), and the hold must last several times $\lambda_2$. Equal steps small enough for the ramp would need thousands of steps; TimeStepping grows the step by the factor grow after every step that converged easily, up to Δtmax, and tstops makes a step end exactly at the end of the ramp. The voltage increments of the ramp are small and the response is nearly linear in $V^2$, so Newton's method converges without the Dirichlet projection of tutorial 02 (ProjectDirichlet=true gives the same results with fewer iterations, but compiles and factorises a second operator: here it costs more than it saves).

λ₁, λ₂ = let b = μ∞ * (τ₁ + τ₂) + μ₁ * τ₁ + μ₂ * τ₂, c = τ₁ * τ₂ * (μ∞ + μ₁ + μ₂)   # retardation times (above)
  sort([(b - sqrt(b^2 - 4μ∞ * c)) / (2μ∞), (b + sqrt(b^2 - 4μ∞ * c)) / (2μ∞)])
end
@printf("relaxation times τ = %.2f s, %.2f s; retardation (creep) times λ = %.2f s, %.2f s\n", τ₁, τ₂, λ₁, λ₂)
t_end = FAST ? 250.0 : 400.0                                   # s: about 6 λ₂ (FAST) and 10 λ₂
Δtmax = 10.0
stepping = FAST ? TimeStepping(; Δt=0.1, t_end, grow=1.3, Δtmax, tstops=(t_r,), maxbisec=4) :
                  TimeStepping(; Δt=0.05, t_end, grow=1.1, Δtmax, tstops=(t_r,), maxbisec=4)
HyperFEMKernels.reset_counters!()
t_solve = @elapsed x, flag = solve!(creep; stepping, post=post)
work = HyperFEMKernels.counters()
nsteps = length(times)
t_step = sort(diff(wall))[cld(nsteps - 1, 2)]                  # median wall-clock time of a step (the first compiles)
@printf("%d steps in %.1f s (%.1f ms per step, median)\n", nsteps, t_solve, 1e3 * t_step)

Long-time limit: the equilibrium branch alone

A static electro-mechanical problem (tutorial 04) with only the neo-Hookean branch, at the final voltage, on the same mesh: the deflection that the creep tends to. It has its own conditions (the voltage follows the load factor) and trial spaces; the test spaces are the same.

equilibrium = ElectroMechModel(mechano=NeoHookean3D(λ=λ, μ=μ∞), electro=IdealDielectric(ε=ε))
Dφ_eq = BoundaryConditions(BoundaryCondition("bottom", 0.0), BoundaryCondition("mid", V0, Λ -> Λ))
D_eq = MultiFieldBC([Du, Dφ_eq])
U_eq = MultiFieldFESpace([TrialFESpace(Vu, D_eq[1], 0.0), TrialFESpace(Vφ, D_eq[2], 0.0)])
res_eq(Λ) = (x, y) -> residual(equilibrium, k, x, y, dΩ)
jac_eq(Λ) = (x, dx, y) -> jacobian(equilibrium, k, x, dx, y, dΩ)
static = StaticNonlinearModel(res_eq, jac_eq, U_eq, V, D_eq; nls=NewtonRaphson(LUSolver(); maxiter=20, atol=1e-12, rtol=1e-9),
                              backend=:compiled)
_, flag_eq = solve!(static; stepping=LoadStepping(nsteps=10, maxbisec=4), ProjectDirichlet=true)
tip_eq = sum(∫(get_state(static)[1] ⋅ ez)dΓtip) / (W * H)

Results

The deflection at the end of the ramp, and then along the hold, against the long-time limit.

i_r = findfirst(t -> t ≥ t_r - 1e-12, times)                  # the step that ends the ramp
for i in unique([1:min(nsteps, 3); i_r; i_r+3:5:nsteps; nsteps])
  @printf("t = %8.3f s   V = %.4f   tip u_z = %.4f mm   (%.1f %% of the long-time value)\n",
          times[i], voltage[i], 1e3 * tip_uz[i], 100 * tip_uz[i] / tip_eq)
end
@printf("equilibrium branch alone at V0: tip u_z = %.4f mm\n", 1e3 * tip_eq)

Checks

Long-time limit. After the hold, the deflection is that of the equilibrium branch alone. What is left of the viscous stresses decays like $e^{-t/\lambda_2}$ (a little more slowly with the backward-Euler update and large steps): after $t_{end}$ it is below 0.5 % (FAST) and 0.05 %.

rel_limit = abs(tip_uz[end] - tip_eq) / abs(tip_eq)
println("long-time limit: |u_z(t_end) − u_z,eq| / |u_z,eq| = $(rel_limit)")
@assert flag == 1 && flag_eq == 1 "a time loop or the load stepping did not finish"
@assert rel_limit < (FAST ? 5e-3 : 5e-4) "the deflection after the hold is not the equilibrium deflection ($(rel_limit))"

Creep time. During the hold the distance to the limit, $r = u_{z,eq} - u_z$, decays with the longest retardation time. With steps of constant size $\Delta t$ the backward-Euler update multiplies it by $1/(1 + \Delta t/\lambda)$ per step, so ten steps of size Δtmax give a measured $\lambda$, which we compare with $\lambda_2$ of the small-strain model (5 %: the deflection is about a tenth of the length, and the electric load changes a little with the deformation).

r = tip_eq .- tip_uz
iconst = findall(d -> abs(d - Δtmax) < 1e-9, diff(times)) .+ 1   # steps of size Δtmax
ia = first(iconst)
ib = min(ia + 10, last(iconst))
λ_measured = Δtmax / ((r[ia] / r[ib])^(1 / (ib - ia)) - 1)
@printf("creep time measured between t = %.1f s and %.1f s: %.2f s (small-strain λ₂ = %.2f s)\n",
        times[ia], times[ib], λ_measured, λ₂)
@assert ib - ia ≥ 5 && times[ib] - times[ia] ≥ 50 "the hold is too short to measure the creep time"
@assert isapprox(λ_measured, λ₂; rtol=0.05) "creep time $(λ_measured) s away from λ₂ = $(λ₂) s"

Creep and the instantaneous response. After the ramp the voltage is constant and the deflection grows monotonically towards the limit, from below. The compliance $u_z/V^2$, relative to that of the equilibrium branch, grows during the whole run, ramp included: the plate is stiffest at the start, where the viscous branches have not relaxed and the response is close to the instantaneous one, $\mu_\infty/(\mu_\infty + \mu_1 + \mu_2) = 0.135$ in the small-strain model (the first step already lets them relax a little).

hold = findfirst(t -> t ≥ t_r - 1e-12, times):nsteps
compliance = (tip_uz ./ voltage .^ 2) ./ (tip_eq / V0^2)
c_inst = μ∞ / (μ∞ + μ₁ + μ₂)
@printf("relative compliance: %.3f after the first step, %.3f at the end of the ramp (instantaneous %.3f)\n",
        compliance[1], compliance[first(hold)], c_inst)
@assert tip_uz[end] > 0 "the plate does not bend upwards"
@assert all(diff(tip_uz[hold]) .> 0) "the deflection does not grow monotonically during the hold"
@assert all(tip_uz[hold] .< tip_eq) "the deflection goes beyond the long-time limit"
@assert all(diff(compliance) .> 0) "the compliance does not grow in time"
@assert compliance[1] < 1.25 * c_inst "the response of the first step is not close to the instantaneous one"

Efficiency. HyperFEMKernels.work_summary lists the groups of integrals of the compiled operator, the path each takes (:linear_maps: the coefficients come from linear maps of the state inputs, the fast path) and whether a part falls back to Gridap. The counters since reset_counters!() show that the forms were only rebound to the new previous state and internal variables at every step (update_fast, one per step) and never compiled again (update_full). The forms were generated when the model was built; during the loop the functions generated (codegen) are two per step: the update of the internal variables of each branch is generated again at every call from the same expression, which finds the compiled code already there (about 1 ms per step, against tens of milliseconds for the Newton iterations). HyperFEM 0.3.0 has no accessor for the operator of a TransientModel: creep.caches.op.inner is an internal field and may change.

ws = HyperFEMKernels.work_summary(creep.caches.op.inner)
foreach(g -> println("  ", g.kind, " group ", g.group, ": ", g.cells, " cells × ", g.points, " points, path ", g.path), ws.groups)
println("  Gridap fallbacks: residual $(ws.fallback_residual), Jacobian $(ws.fallback_jacobian); ",
        "updates: $(work.update_fast) fast, $(work.update_full) full; functions generated: $(work.codegen)")
@assert ws.fallback_residual == 0 && ws.fallback_jacobian == 0 "part of the forms is assembled by Gridap"
@assert work.update_full == 0 "the compiled forms were compiled again during the time loop"
@assert work.update_fast == nsteps "the compiled forms were not rebound once per step"

Going further

  • Hold the voltage for a short time and switch it off (BoundaryCondition("mid", V0, t -> …) with any function of time): the plate does not come back at once, it recovers with the same relaxation times.
  • Raise $V_0$: the equilibrium deflection grows as $V_0^2$ up to $V_0 \approx 0.012$ (about 13 mm), and beyond that the plate curls up as it creeps (with $V_0 = 0.02$ the tip of the equilibrium plate rises by about 30 mm, 60 % of its length). More voltage steps (a smaller Δt or grow) may be needed for the large rotations.
  • Add a third branch with a long relaxation time (the HyperFEM test of a viscoelastic bar uses $\tau_3 = 500$ s), or use ViscousIncompressible(NeoHookean3D(...); τ) branches.
  • Solve the two fields one after the other (StaggeredModel, tutorial 09) instead of together.
